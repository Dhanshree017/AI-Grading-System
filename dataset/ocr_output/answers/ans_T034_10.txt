A-1) Explain the concept of model generalization and why it is important in machine learning 
Model generalization is the ability of a machine learning model to correctly predict outcomes for new, 
unseen data that was not part of the training set. The main objective of any learning algorithm is not to 
perform well on the training data, but to learn the true underlying patterns that exist in the data so that it 
can make reliable predictions in the real world. 
A model with good generalization captures the essential relationships between variables without memorizing 
noise. When a model becomes too complex and learns every small variation in the training data, it results in 
overfitting, which reduces its performance on new data. On the other hand, if the model is too simple, it 
fails to learn important patterns, resulting in underfitting. Techniques such as cross-validation, 
regularization, and proper dataset splitting help in improving generalization. 
Generalization is important because real-world data is always different from training data. In applications 
such as medical diagnosis, financial risk analysis, and autonomous systems, poor generalization can lead to 
wrong decisions, financial losses, or safety risks. Therefore, a well-generalized model ensures accuracy, 
reliability, and real-world usability. 
A-2) Describe the role of data labeling in supervised learning systems 
Data labeling is the process of assigning correct and meaningful output values to training data in supervised 
learning. These labels act as a reference or ground truth that guides the learning algorithm in understanding 
how input features relate to desired outputs. Without labeled data, supervised learning models cannot learn 
the mapping between inputs and outputs. 
For example, in image recognition, images are labeled as “cat,” “dog,” or “car.” In medical diagnosis, 
patient records may be labeled as “disease present” or “disease absent.” The model uses these labels to 
detect patterns and relationships within the data. Accurate labeling enables the model to learn correct 
decision boundaries, while incorrect or biased labels lead to wrong predictions and unfair models. 
Thus, data labeling directly influences model accuracy, reliability, and fairness. High-quality labeled data is 
often more valuable than large quantities of poorly labeled data in supervised machine learning.
