A-a. Explain the concept of learning bias in machine learning. Discuss different sources of 
bias and their impact on model generalization. 
Definition of Learning Bias 
Learning bias in machine learning refers to systematic errors introduced into a model due to 
incorrect assumptions, limitations in data, or design choices that influence how the model 
learns patterns. 
Sources of Learning Bias and Their Impact 
1. Data Collection Bias – Occurs when training data does not represent real-world 
scenarios, leading to poor predictions. 
2. Sampling Bias – Over-representation or under-representation of certain groups affects 
fairness and accuracy. 
3. Algorithmic Bias – Bias introduced by the learning algorithm due to built-in 
assumptions. 
4. Measurement Bias – Errors caused by inaccurate or inconsistent data measurements. 
5. Labeling Bias – Human errors during data annotation introduce incorrect patterns. 
6. Historical Bias – Bias inherited from historical data reflecting social or systemic 
inequalities. 
7. Feature Selection Bias – Choosing inappropriate or incomplete features limits 
learning capability. 
8. Impact on Generalization – Biased models perform well on training data but fail on 
unseen data. 
9. Reduced Fairness – Bias can cause unfair decisions in sensitive applications. 
10. Overall Effect – Learning bias reduces model reliability and generalization 
performance. 
A-b. Describe the role of assumptions in machine learning algorithms. Explain how 
assumptions influence model selection and performance. 
Definition of Assumptions in Machine Learning 
Assumptions are predefined beliefs about data distribution, relationships, or patterns that 
guide how a machine learning algorithm learns from data. 
Role and Influence of Assumptions 
1. Simplifies Learning Process – Assumptions reduce complexity of learning. 
2. Linear Assumptions – Linear models assume a linear relationship between features 
and output. 
3. Independence Assumption – Naive Bayes assumes features are independent. 
4. Data Distribution Assumption – Some models assume normal distribution of data. 
5. Influence on Model Choice – Incorrect assumptions lead to wrong model selection. 
6. Bias Introduction – Strong assumptions increase bias in the model. 
7. Performance Impact – Models fail if assumptions do not match real data. 
8. Generalization Effect – Proper assumptions improve generalization. 
9. Flexibility vs Simplicity – Complex models make fewer assumptions but risk 
overfitting. 
10. Conclusion – Understanding assumptions is essential for effective ML performance. 
A-c. Explain the concept of generalization in machine learning. Discuss why a model that 
performs well on training data may fail on unseen data. 
Definition of Generalization 
Generalization refers to the ability of a machine learning model to perform well on new, 
unseen data after being trained on a training dataset. 
Explanation of Generalization Failure 
1. Overfitting – Model memorizes training data instead of learning patterns. 
2. High Model Complexity – Excessively complex models capture noise. 
3. Insufficient Training Data – Limited data leads to poor pattern learning. 
4. Data Leakage – Training data contains information from test data. 
5. Poor Feature Selection – Irrelevant features harm generalization. 
6. Noise in Data – Noisy data misguides learning. 
7. Bias–Variance Tradeoff – High variance reduces generalization ability. 
8. Distribution Shift – Training and test data distributions differ. 
9. Lack of Regularization – No penalty for complexity increases overfitting. 
10. Conclusion – Generalization depends on balanced model complexity and quality data. 
A-d. Discuss the importance of data quality in machine learning systems. Explain how 
noise, missing values, and inconsistencies affect learning outcomes. 
Definition of Data Quality 
Data quality refers to the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and reliability of data used to 
train machine learning models. 
Importance and Effects of Poor Data Quality 
1. Noise in Data – Random errors confuse the learning process. 
2. Missing Values – Incomplete data leads to biased predictions. 
3. Inconsistent Data – Conflicting values reduce model reliability. 
4. Reduced Accuracy – Poor data quality directly lowers performance. 
5. Increased Overfitting – Models learn noise instead of patterns. 
6. Slower Training – Noisy data increases computation time. 
7. Unstable Models – Small changes in data cause large prediction changes. 
8. Poor Generalization – Model fails on real-world data. 
9. Higher Maintenance Cost – Frequent retraining is required. 
10. Conclusion – High-quality data is essential for effective ML systems.
