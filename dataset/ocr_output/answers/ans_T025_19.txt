A-1. Explain the concept of data preprocessing in machine learning. Discuss various 
preprocessing tasks and explain how they influence model performance. 
Definition of Data Preprocessing 
Data preprocessing is a crucial step in machine learning that involves preparing raw data into 
a clean, structured, and usable format before applying learning algorithms. 
Preprocessing Tasks and Their Influence 
1. Handling Missing Values – Missing data is handled using techniques such as deletion 
or imputation to avoid biased learning. 
2. Noise Removal – Eliminating incorrect or inconsistent data improves model 
reliability. 
3. Outlier Detection – Removing extreme values prevents distortion of model 
predictions. 
4. Data Normalization – Scaling data to a common range improves convergence of 
algorithms. 
5. Standardization – Ensures features have zero mean and unit variance. 
6. Categorical Encoding – Converts non-numeric data into numeric form usable by 
models. 
7. Data Integration – Combines multiple datasets to create a unified view. 
8. Feature Transformation – Enhances patterns through log or power transformations. 
9. Data Reduction – Reduces data size while retaining important information. 
10. Impact on Performance – Proper preprocessing improves accuracy, speed, and 
generalization ability of ML models. 
A-2. Describe the working of a Decision Tree algorithm. Explain how decisions are 
made and discuss its advantages and limitations. 
Definition of Decision Tree 
A Decision Tree is a supervised learning algorithm that represents decision-making using a 
tree-like structure of nodes and branches. 
Working of Decision Tree Algorithm 
1. Root Node Creation – The most informative feature is selected as the root. 
2. Splitting Criteria – Data is split using metrics like Information Gain or Gini Index. 
3. Recursive Partitioning – Each child node is further divided. 
4. Decision Rules – Each internal node represents a decision condition. 
5. Leaf Nodes – Represent final class labels or predicted outputs. 
6. Stopping Conditions – Tree growth stops based on depth or minimum samples. 
7. Prediction Process – New data follows paths from root to leaf. 
8. Advantages – Easy to interpret and visualize. 
9. Limitations – Sensitive to noise and prone to overfitting. 
10. Use Cases – Widely used in classification and regression tasks. 
A-3. Explain the bias–variance tradeoff. Discuss its impact on underfitting and 
overfitting with practical illustrations. 
Definition of Bias–Variance Tradeoff 
The bias–variance tradeoff explains the balance between a model’s simplicity and 
complexity that affects its predictive performance. 
Explanation 
1. Bias – Error due to overly simplistic assumptions. 
2. Variance – Error caused by sensitivity to training data. 
3. High Bias Models – Lead to underfitting. 
4. High Variance Models – Lead to overfitting. 
5. Underfitting – Poor performance on both training and test data. 
6. Overfitting – Excellent training accuracy but poor test performance. 
7. Practical Example – Linear vs high-degree polynomial models. 
8. Tradeoff Nature – Reducing bias increases variance and vice versa. 
9. Goal – Minimize total error. 
10. Solution – Choose optimal model complexity. 
A-4. Discuss the importance of model evaluation in machine learning. Explain why 
accuracy alone is not sufficient and describe other evaluation measures. 
Definition of Model Evaluation 
Model evaluation assesses how well a trained model performs on unseen data. 
Importance and Evaluation Measures 
1. Performance Measurement – Determines model effectiveness. 
2. Generalization Check – Ensures real-world usability. 
3. Accuracy Limitation – Misleading in imbalanced datasets. 
4. Precision – Measures correctness of positive predictions. 
5. Recall – Measures ability to detect actual positives. 
6. F1-Score – Balances precision and recall. 
7. Confusion Matrix – Provides detailed error analysis. 
8. ROC Curve – Shows tradeoff between true and false positives. 
9. AUC – Indicates overall classification performance. 
10. Conclusion – Multiple metrics give better evaluation.
