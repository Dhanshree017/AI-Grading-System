A-1. Give 5 Domains of AI 
1. Machine Learning (ML): 
Focuses on building models that learn patterns from data and improve performance 
without explicit programming. Includes supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement 
learning techniques. 
2. Natural Language Processing (NLP): 
Deals with enabling machines to understand, generate, and interpret human language. 
Used in chatbots, translation systems, and sentiment analysis. 
3. Computer Vision: 
Enables machines to interpret images and videos. Used in facial recognition, medical 
imaging, object detection, and autonomous driving. 
4. Robotics: 
Integrates AI into physical machines to perform tasks such as navigation, 
manipulation, and interaction. Includes autonomous robots and industrial automation. 
5. Expert Systems: 
AI systems built using domain knowledge and rule-based reasoning to provide expert
level decisions, common in medical diagnosis and financial analysis. 
A-2. What is data preprocessing and why is it important? 
Data preprocessing is the series of operations performed to transform raw, messy data into a 
structured and usable format for machine learning. It includes cleaning, handling missing 
values, encoding categorical features, scaling numerical values, noise removal, and 
normalization. 
Importance: 
 Ensures data consistency and reliability. 
 Reduces noise and errors that degrade model accuracy. 
 Helps models converge faster and perform better. 
 Prevents bias caused by outliers or imbalanced distributions. 
 Improves the model’s ability to generalize to unseen data. 
A-3. What are hyperparameters? Give two examples. 
Hyperparameters are external configuration parameters set before training a model, 
controlling how the algorithm learns. They are not learned from data but manually chosen or 
optimized. 
Examples: 
 Learning Rate (Neural Networks): Determines how quickly weights are updated. 
 Max Depth (Decision Tree): Controls the maximum allowable tree expansion. 
A-4. Types of Clustering 
1. K-Means Clustering: 
Divides data into k clusters by minimizing within-cluster variance. Works well for 
spherical, well-separated groups. 
2. Hierarchical Clustering: 
Builds a tree-like structure (dendrogram) either bottom-up (agglomerative) or top
down (divisive), useful for nested clusters. 
3. DBSCAN: 
Groups data points based on density; identifies noise and suitable for non-linear 
clusters. 
4. Gaussian Mixture Models (GMM): 
Assumes data is distributed as a mixture of multiple Gaussian distributions; captures 
soft cluster assignments. 
A-5. What is classification and regression? Give examples. 
Classification: Predicts discrete categories. 
Example: Email spam detection (spam/not spam). 
Regression: Predicts continuous numeric values. 
Example: Predicting house prices based on area and location. 
A-6. Advantages and Disadvantages of Decision Trees 
Advantages: 
 Easy to visualize and interpret. 
 Handles numerical and categorical data. 
 Requires little data preprocessing. 
 Provides clear feature importance. 
Disadvantages: 
 Prone to overfitting with deep trees. 
 Instability: small data changes may alter structure. 
 Not ideal for continuous predictions unless ensemble methods are used. 
A-7. How to make a model underfit and overfit 
Underfitting: 
 Use overly simple model (e.g., linear model for non-linear data). 
 Reduce training time excessively. 
 Remove essential features or use too much regularization. 
Overfitting: 
 Train too many epochs. 
 Use deep, complex models with insufficient data. 
 Avoid regularization and dropout. 
 Include noise in training data.

SECTION B —  
B-1. Challenges in ML and solutions 
Imbalanced Data: 
 Issue: Dominant class biases the model. 
 Solution: SMOTE, oversampling, undersampling, class-weighted loss. 
Missing Values: 
 Issue: Reduces reliability. 
 Solution: Mean/median imputation, KNN imputation, interpolation, or deletion. 
Curse of Dimensionality: 
 Issue: Too many features reduce model efficiency. 
 Solution: PCA, feature selection, domain-driven feature reduction. 
Concept Drift: 
 Issue: Data patterns change over time. 
 Solution: Incremental learning, retraining, sliding window models. 
B-2. What is Regularization? Explain its types. 
Regularization prevents overfitting by penalizing model complexity. 
Types: 
 L1 Regularization (Lasso): Adds absolute weight penalty; performs feature selection. 
 L2 Regularization (Ridge): Penalizes squared weights; smoothens model. 
 Elastic Net: Combination of L1 & L2. 
 Dropout: Randomly deactivates neurons during training to prevent co-adaptation. 
 Early Stopping: Stops training when validation loss increases. 
B-3. Ensemble Learning — Bagging & Boosting 
Ensemble Learning improves performance by combining multiple models. 
Bagging: 
 Builds models independently on random subsets. 
 Reduces variance. 
 Example: Random Forest. 
Boosting: 
 Models are trained sequentially, each correcting previous errors. 
 Reduces bias. 
 Examples: AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost.
