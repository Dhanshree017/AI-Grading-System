A-2. What is data preprocessing and why is it important? 
Definition: sequence of transformations applied to raw inputs to make them consistent, 
informative and suitable for modelling (cleaning, transforming, encoding). 
Importance: reduces noise and bias, ensures numerical stability, enables meaningful feature 
comparisons (scaling/normalization), handles missing or inconsistent values, and often 
increases predictive performance more than marginal model changes. Proper preprocessing 
is prerequisite for reproducible and robust ML. 
A-3. What are hyperparameters? Give two examples 
Definition: external configuration knobs chosen before learning that control model structure 
or optimizer behavior; they are not estimated from training by gradient descent. 
Examples: (1) Learning rate (η) controlling step-size in gradient updates; (2) 
Regularization coefficient (λ) that penalizes large weights (L2) or controls sparsity (L1). 
A-4. Explain types of clustering 
 Partitioning (K-means / K-medoids): iterative assignment to k centroids/medoids 
minimizing within-cluster variance. Good for spherical clusters. 
 Hierarchical (Agglomerative / Divisive): builds tree of clusters (dendrogram) useful 
when cluster granularity unknown. 
 Density-based (DBSCAN / HDBSCAN): identifies clusters as high-density regions; 
finds arbitrary shapes and outliers. 
 Model-based (Gaussian Mixture Models): probabilistic clusters with soft 
assignments via EM; models covariance structure. 
 Spectral / Graph-based: uses eigenvectors of affinity/Laplacian to separate non
convex manifolds. 
A-5. What is classification and regression? Explain with examples 
Classification: mapping inputs to discrete categories. Example: diagnosing images as 
{benign, malignant}. Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC. 
Regression: mapping inputs to continuous targets. Example: forecasting monthly demand 
(units). Metrics: MAE, RMSE, R². Choice of loss (MSE vs MAE) reflects tolerance to 
outliers.

B-3. Ensemble Learning — Bagging and Boosting (with examples) 
Principle: combine multiple models to reduce error components (variance, bias) and 
increase robustness. 
Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating) 
 Mechanism: train base learners independently on bootstrap samples; aggregate 
predictions (averaging/voting). 
 Effect: lowers variance by averaging uncorrelated errors. 
 Example: Random Forest — bagged decision trees with additional random feature 
selection per split; strong off-the-shelf performance and resilience to overfitting. 
Boosting 
 Mechanism: sequentially fit weak learners where each new learner focuses on 
residuals or misclassified instances of the ensemble so far. 
 Effect: reduces bias and often variance when regularized; converts many weak learners 
into a strong predictor. 
 Examples: AdaBoost (reweights examples), Gradient Boosting (fit negative gradients; 
implementations: XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost). Regularization (shrinkage, tree 
constraints) crucial to prevent overfitting. 
Stacking & Blending: meta-learner trained on base model predictions to capture 
complementary strengths. 
B-4. Entire ML pipeline and model evaluation 
Pipeline steps (detailed): 
1. Problem framing & metric selection: define business objective and select 
appropriate metrics (e.g., recall vs precision tradeoff). 
2. Data engineering: ingestion, schema design, sampling strategy, labeling pipeline. 
3. EDA & diagnostics: distribution checks, feature correlations, outlier detection, label 
noise estimation. 
4. Preprocessing & featurization: cleaning, imputing, encoding, scaling, text/image 
preprocessing, embedding extraction. 
5. Feature engineering: interactions, aggregations, temporal features, domain 
transformations, feature selection. 
6. Modeling: choose algorithm family; baseline models; iterate complexity. 
7. Validation & hyperparameter search: cross-validation, time series CV, nested CV 
for honest estimates; tune with grid/random/Bayesian search. 
8. Evaluation: on holdout/test set; compute chosen metrics; analyze calibration and error 
slices. 
9. Deployment: packaging, serving, latency and throughput considerations, CI/CD. 
10. Monitoring: production metrics, drift detection, data quality checks, feedback 
capture. 
11. Governance: logging, versioning, explainability reports, privacy compliance. 
Evaluation best practices: use metrics aligned to cost of errors, confusion matrix analysis 
for classification, calibration curves for probabilistic outputs, significance testing for model 
comparisons, and business A/B testing for real-world impact. 
B-5. Types of data ML uses & qualities of good data 
Types: 
 Tabular / structured — spreadsheets, relational rows. 
 Text — documents, logs, social posts. 
 Images / video — pixels, frames, spatio-temporal streams. 
 Audio / speech — waveforms, spectrograms. 
 Time-series — sensor logs, financial series. 
 Graph — nodes & edges (social networks, molecules). 
 Spatial / geospatial — coordinates, maps. 
Qualities of good data: 
 Accuracy & label correctness (low label noise). 
 Completeness (minimal missingness for core features). 
 Representativeness (covers deployment population and edge cases). 
 Consistency (units/formats, no contradictions). 
 Sufficient diversity and volume (adequate examples per class/segment). 
 Low measurement noise / high signal-to-noise ratio. 
 Freshness (timely for non-stationary problems). 
 Metadata & provenance (feature definitions, collection method) for reproducibility. 
B-6. Naïve Bayes and SVM — detailed note 
Naïve Bayes (NB) 
 Core idea: apply Bayes’ theorem with conditional independence: Variants: 
Multinomial NB (counts/text), Bernoulli NB (binary features), Gaussian NB 
(continuous). 
 Pros: extremely fast, low memory, works well with high-dim sparse features (text), 
interpretable priors. 
 Cons: independence assumption often violated; cannot capture feature interactions; 
probability estimates crude unless calibrated. 
 Use cases: spam detection, document classification, simple baselines. 
Support Vector Machine (SVM) 
 Core idea: find hyperplane maximizing margin between classes; soft margin 
introduces slack ξ and regularization C to trade margin vs misclassification. 
 Kernel trick: map to high-dim feature space implicitly with kernel 
K(x,x′)K(x,x')K(x,x′) (RBF, polynomial) to enable non-linear decision boundaries. 
 Optimization: convex quadratic program (global optimum), can be solved in primal 
or dual. 
 Pros: robust in high dimensional settings, effective with clear margins, strong 
theoretical guarantees. 
 Cons: computationally heavy for very large n, kernel selection & hyperparameter 
tuning (C, γ) crucial, limited probabilistic outputs (need calibration via Platt scaling). 
 Use cases: text classification (linear SVMs), image recognition on moderate datasets, 
bioinformatics.
