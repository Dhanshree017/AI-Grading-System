A-a. Explain the concept of model robustness in machine learning. 
Model robustness in machine learning is a model's ability to maintain stable, accurate 
performance despite real-world challenges like noisy data, unexpected shifts in data 
distribution (e.g., lighting changes, different user groups), or deliberate adversarial attacks, 
ensuring it generalizes reliably beyond its training set, not just performing well on clean, 
familiar data. It's crucial for trustworthy AI in safety-critical systems (like self-driving cars) 
where brittle, high-accuracy-but-fragile models can fail catastrophically.  
Key Aspects of Robustness 
 Generalization: Goes beyond high accuracy on training data to performing well on unseen 
data from different, real-world conditions. 
 Resistance to Perturbations: Handles noise, missing data, slight rotations, or faint images 
without significant performance drops, unlike models prone to overfitting. 
 Data Distribution Shifts: Adapts to changes like seasonal variations in weather for image 
recognition or evolving user behaviors over time (data drift). 
 Adversarial Attacks: Stays resilient against malicious inputs designed to fool it (e.g., small 
pixel changes to misclassify an image).  
Why It Matters 
 Trust & Safety: Essential for AI in autonomous vehicles, medical diagnostics, and finance, 
where errors have serious consequences. 
 Real-World Reliability: Ensures models work consistently in dynamic environments, not 
just controlled lab settings.  
How It Differs from Accuracy 
 Accuracy: Measures performance on clean, typical test data (e.g., 99% on standard digits). 
 Robustness: Measures performance under stress (e.g., faint, rotated digits or adversarial 
examples). A model can be accurate but not robust (brittle).  
Examples of Data Shifts 
 Natural Shifts: Different lighting, camera angles, or new types of objects (e.g., recognizing 
an unfamiliar fruit). 
 Adversarial Shifts: Intentional small modifications to input data to cause misclassification. 
 Performative Shifts: Changes in user behavior because of the model's predictions, creating 
feedback loops.  
In essence, a robust model doesn't just learn patterns; it learns the underlying concepts well 
enough to handle the unpredictable nature of the real world.
