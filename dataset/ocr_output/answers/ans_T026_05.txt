A-b. Describe the role of assumptions in machine learning algorithms. Explain how assumptions 
influence model selection and performance. 
Definition of Assumptions in Machine Learning 
Assumptions are predefined beliefs about data distribution, relationships, or patterns that guide how a 
machine learning algorithm learns from data. 
Role and Influence of Assumptions 
1. Simplifies Learning Process – Assumptions reduce complexity of learning. 
2. Linear Assumptions – Linear models assume a linear relationship between features and output. 
3. Independence Assumption – Naive Bayes assumes features are independent. 
4. Data Distribution Assumption – Some models assume normal distribution of data. 
5. Influence on Model Choice – Incorrect assumptions lead to wrong model selection. 
6. Bias Introduction – Strong assumptions increase bias in the model. 
7. Performance Impact – Models fail if assumptions do not match real data. 
8. Generalization Effect – Proper assumptions improve generalization. 
9. Flexibility vs Simplicity – Complex models make fewer assumptions but risk overfitting. 
10. Conclusion – Understanding assumptions is essential for effective ML performance. 
A-c. Explain the concept of generalization in machine learning. Discuss why a model that performs well 
on training data may fail on unseen data. 
Definition of Generalization 
Generalization refers to the ability of a machine learning model to perform well on new, unseen data after 
being trained on a training dataset. 
Explanation of Generalization Failure 
1. Overfitting – Model memorizes training data instead of learning patterns. 
2. High Model Complexity – Excessively complex models capture noise. 
3. Insufficient Training Data – Limited data leads to poor pattern learning. 
4. Data Leakage – Training data contains information from test data. 
5. Poor Feature Selection – Irrelevant features harm generalization. 
6. Noise in Data – Noisy data misguides learning. 
7. Bias–Variance Tradeoff – High variance reduces generalization ability. 
8. Distribution Shift – Training and test data distributions differ. 
9. Lack of Regularization – No penalty for complexity increases overfitting. 
10. Conclusion – Generalization depends on balanced model complexity and quality data. 
A-d. Discuss the importance of data quality in machine learning systems. Explain how noise, missing 
values, and inconsistencies affect learning outcomes. 
Definition of Data Quality 
Data quality refers to the accuracy, completeness, consistency, and reliability of data used to train machine 
learning models. 
Importance and Effects of Poor Data Quality 
1. Noise in Data – Random errors confuse the learning process. 
2. Missing Values – Incomplete data leads to biased predictions. 
3. Inconsistent Data – Conflicting values reduce model reliability. 
4. Reduced Accuracy – Poor data quality directly lowers performance. 
5. Increased Overfitting – Models learn noise instead of patterns. 
6. Slower Training – Noisy data increases computation time. 
7. Unstable Models – Small changes in data cause large prediction changes. 
8. Poor Generalization – Model fails on real-world data. 
9. Higher Maintenance Cost – Frequent retraining is required. 
10. Conclusion – High-quality data is essential for effective ML systems. 
A-e. Explain the difference between shallow learning and deep learning approaches. Discuss their 
characteristics, strengths, and limitations in theory. 
Definition 
Shallow learning uses models with limited layers, while deep learning uses multi-layer neural networks to 
learn complex representations. 
Comparison and Discussion 
1. Model Structure – Shallow models have few layers; deep models have many layers. 
2. Feature Engineering – Shallow learning relies on manual features. 
3. Automatic Feature Learning – Deep learning extracts features automatically. 
4. Data Requirement – Deep learning requires large datasets. 
5. Computational Cost – Deep models need high computational power. 
6. Interpretability – Shallow models are easier to interpret. 
7. Accuracy – Deep learning performs better on complex tasks. 
8. Training Time – Shallow models train faster. 
9. Use Cases – Shallow learning for simple problems, deep learning for vision and NLP. 
10. Limitations – Deep learning risks overfitting and lacks transparency. 
A-f. Describe the ethical considerations involved in developing machine learning systems. Explain issues 
such as fairness, transparency, accountability, and privacy. 
Definition of Ethical Considerations 
Ethical considerations in machine learning involve responsible development and deployment of models to 
avoid harm and ensure trust. 
Key Ethical Issues 
1. Fairness – Models must not discriminate against groups. 
2. Bias Reduction – Biased data leads to unfair outcomes. 
3. Transparency – Decisions should be explainable. 
4. Interpretability – Users should understand model behavior. 
5. Accountability – Responsibility for model decisions must be defined. 
6. Privacy Protection – Sensitive data must be secured. 
7. Data Consent – Users should know how data is used. 
8. Security Risks – Models can be misused or attacked. 
9. Regulatory Compliance – Laws and guidelines must be followed. 
10. Conclusion – Ethical ML ensures trust, safety, and social acceptance.
