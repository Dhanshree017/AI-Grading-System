A-1. Explain the role of assumptions in machine learning models and analyze how incorrect assumptions 
can affect model behavior and decision-making.  
• What are Assumptions? Assumptions are like the "basic rules" a computer follows to learn. Since 
the computer doesn't know anything at the start, we tell it a few rules, like "assume the data follows a 
straight line."  
• Why they are used: They make the work faster. It is like giving the computer a map so it doesn't get 
lost in all the data.  
• The Danger of Wrong Rules: If your data is a big curve but your rule is "it must be a straight line," 
the computer will never get the right answer. This is called Underfitting.  
• Bad Decisions: In the real world, if an AI assumes that "only people with high salaries are good," it 
will make bad decisions by rejecting honest people who just have lower pay. This makes the AI 
unfair and useless.  
A-2. Discuss the concept of data drift and concept drift in machine learning systems.  
• Data Drift (Input Change): This is when the data coming into the model changes.  
o Example: You made an AI to predict car prices in 2020. In 2025, people are driving mostly 
electric cars. The "input" (the types of cars) has drifted or changed.  
• Concept Drift (Meaning Change): This is when the "definition" of something changes.  
o Example: Before a pandemic, "buying 50 rolls of toilet paper" was weird behavior. During 
the pandemic, it became "normal." The "concept" of normal changed.  
• Long-term Impact: If you don't watch out for these, your model will get "stale." It will keep giving 
answers based on old rules, and its accuracy will drop lower and lower until it is just guessing.  
A-4. Describe the challenges of scaling machine learning models from small to large datasets.  
• Computer Power: A small dataset works on a laptop. A large one needs many giant, expensive 
servers. If the data is too big, the computer might run out of memory and crash.  
• Messy Data: When you have 100 rows, it is easy to clean. When you have 100 million rows, there 
are thousands of mistakes, typos, and empty boxes. Cleaning this is a huge headache.  
• Time: Some models take minutes to learn. When you scale up, they might take weeks to finish. This 
makes it hard to fix mistakes quickly.  
• Storage: You need special systems (like "The Cloud") to store all that data so it doesn't get lost or 
corrupted.  
A-5. Explain the importance of validation and testing strategies.  
• Don't Cheat: If you test a student on the same questions they practiced, they will get 100% just by 
memorizing. We use Testing on new data to prove the AI actually learned the logic.  
• K-Fold Strategy: Instead of testing just once, we split the data 5 times and test it 5 times. This 
ensures the model wasn't just "lucky" with one set of data.  
• Accuracy Paradox: If 99% of people are healthy, an AI can be "99% accurate" by just saying 
everyone is healthy. Good testing looks at Precision and Recall to see if the AI can actually find the 
1% who are sick.  
• Safety: Proper testing finds bugs before the AI is used on real people, which prevents dangerous 
mistakes.
