A-3. What is the role of constraints in machine learning algorithms? 
Constraints are formal restrictions or requirements imposed on the learning process to ensure 
the resulting model is useful, safe, and generalizes well. 
 Structural Regularization: Constraints are primarily used for Regularization ($L_1, 
L_2$). By constraining the magnitude of the weight vector ($\|w\|$), we limit the 
model's complexity, effectively preventing it from "memorizing" the training data 
(overfitting). 
 Domain Knowledge Integration: Constraints allow us to inject Inductive Bias or 
physical laws into the model. For instance, in a physics-informed neural network, we 
can constrain the output to obey the Law of Conservation of Energy. 
 Fairness and Ethics: Constraints play a massive role in Algorithmic Fairness. We 
can add constraints to ensure that the model’s predictions are statistically independent 
of protected attributes like race or gender. 
 Operational Feasibility: Hard constraints ensure that model outputs are logically 
sound (e.g., ensuring a probability output stays in the range $[0, 1]$ or that a predicted 
price is never negative). 
 Support Vector Machines (SVM): In SVMs, the role of constraints is central; the 
algorithm solves a Constrained Optimization Problem to find the hyperplane that 
maximizes the margin while correctly classifying points. 
A-4. Explain the difference between model performance and model reliability. 
While performance measures "how well" a model works on a specific dataset, reliability 
measures "how consistently" it works across time and changing conditions. 
 Model Performance (Static Accuracy): This is a quantitative measure of the model's 
predictive power on a fixed test set. Metrics include Accuracy, Precision, Recall, and 
AUC-ROC. It is a snapshot of the model's success in a "controlled" environment. 
 Model Reliability (Dynamic Robustness): Reliability is the probability that the 
model will perform its intended function without failure for a specified period under 
specified conditions. It accounts for Robustness against noise and Resilience against 
data drift. 
 The Generalization Gap: A model with high performance but low reliability often 
has a large "Generalization Gap"—it excels on training/test data but fails on real-world 
"out-of-distribution" data. 
 Drift Sensitivity: A reliable model is one that is resistant to Concept Drift (changes 
in the relationship between $x$ and $y$) and Data Drift (changes in the distribution 
of $x$). 
 Operational Context: High performance is a requirement for research, but high 
reliability is a requirement for Production-Grade AI (e.g., medical diagnostics or 
autonomous flight). 
A-5. Describe how feedback is used to improve machine learning systems. 
Feedback loops are the fundamental mechanism for Continuous Learning and system 
evolution, moving beyond "one-shot" training to iterative refinement. 
 Backpropagation (The Mathematical Loop): At the algorithmic level, the "Loss 
Function" provides feedback. The error is propagated back through the layers to adjust 
weights, minimizing the objective function through successive iterations. 
 Human-in-the-Loop (HITL): Feedback from domain experts (e.g., doctors reviewing 
AI diagnoses) is used to relabel "uncertain" cases. This high-quality feedback is then 
used to retrain the model, significantly improving its precision in edge cases. 
 Reinforcement Learning (RL): In RL, the feedback is a Reward Signal. The system 
learns an optimal policy by maximizing cumulative rewards received from the 
environment after each action. 
 Active Learning: The model identifies instances where its "Confidence Score" is low 
and proactively seeks feedback for those specific points. This makes the learning 
process more efficient by focusing on the most informative data. 
 Model Monitoring & MLOps: In production, user feedback (like "thumbs down" on 
a recommendation) provides real-time data to detect Model Decay. This triggers 
automated retraining pipelines to keep the model aligned with current user behavior. 
 Closing the Virtuous Cycle: Effective feedback loops create a "Data Flywheel"—
better models lead to more users, more users provide more feedback, which in turn 
creates even better models.
