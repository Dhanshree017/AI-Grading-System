A-1. Explain the role of assumptions in machine learning models.  
• What are they? Assumptions are like "rules of thumb" the computer uses to learn. It’s impossible 
for a computer to learn without some basic starting rules.  
• Why do we need them? They make learning faster. For example, a "Linear" model assumes that the 
data follows a straight line. This saves the computer from trying billions of other shapes.  
• The Problem with Wrong Rules: If you assume the data is a straight line, but it’s actually a big 
curve, the computer will give you the wrong answer every time.  
• Decision Making: If the computer’s rules are wrong, its choices will be bad. For example, a bank AI 
might reject good people for loans just because its "rules" are too simple or outdated.  
A-2. Discuss the concept of data drift and concept drift.  
• Data Drift: This is when the "Input" changes.  
o Example: You trained a model to recognize winter clothes, but now it is summer. The pictures 
(input) are totally different now.  
• Concept Drift: This is when the "Definition" or "Meaning" changes. o Example: In the year 2000, 
$50 was "expensive" for a pair of jeans. In 2025, $50 is "cheap." The "concept" of expensive has 
changed.  
• Impact: Both of these make the model "stale" or "dumb" over time.  
• Long-term: If you don't update the model with new data, its accuracy will slowly drop to zero. You 
have to retrain it to keep it fresh.  
A-3. Explain how interpretability and explainability help in the real world.  
• Trust: People don't trust "Magic Boxes." If a doctor uses an AI to find cancer, they need to know 
why the AI chose that spot on the X-ray.  
• Explainability: This is being able to say: "I denied this loan because the person’s debt was too 
high." It gives a clear reason for the answer.  
• Interpretability: This is being able to look inside the AI and see the logic.  
• Adoption: If a company can't explain how its AI works, the government might not let them use it. 
Being able to explain "Why" makes people more comfortable using AI in big jobs like hospitals or 
law.  
A-4. Describe the challenges of scaling from small to large datasets.  
• Slow Speed: A model that takes 1 minute to learn 100 rows might take 10 days to learn 1 million 
rows. This is a huge speed problem.  
• Expensive Computers: Small data fits on a laptop. Big data needs giant, expensive "Cloud" servers 
with many GPUs.  
• Dirty Data: Big datasets are almost always "messy." They have many mistakes, empty boxes, and 
duplicate info. Cleaning this takes a lot of work.  
• Memory: Sometimes the data is so big it won't even fit in the computer's memory at once, so you 
have to break it into small "batches."
