A-1. What is data augmentation? Explain its importance in deep learning. 
Data augmentation is a technique used to artificially increase the quantity and diversity of training data by 
applying various transformations to existing samples. In deep learning, especially for image and audio tasks, 
models perform better when they are trained on large and varied datasets. Data augmentation helps achieve 
this without needing to collect new real-world data. Common image augmentation operations include 
rotating an image, flipping it horizontally or vertically, cropping certain regions, adjusting brightness or 
contrast, adding noise, and scaling or warping. These operations allow the model to see many slightly 
changed versions of the same image, forcing it to learn general patterns rather than memorizing specific 
pixel arrangements. The importance of data augmentation is that it reduces overfitting by preventing the 
model from becoming too specialized to the training set. It improves generalization because the model learns 
to recognize objects or features under different viewpoints and lighting conditions. It also makes the dataset 
more balanced and robust, especially when the original dataset is small. In many computer vision tasks like 
classification, object detection, and segmentation, augmentation significantly improves accuracy. 
A-3. What is a decision boundary? Give an intuitive explanation with an example. 
A decision boundary is a surface or line that separates different classes in the feature space used by a 
classification model. It represents the dividing rule learned by the model to distinguish between categories. 
Intuitively, suppose you have a dataset with two features: height and weight, and you want to classify 
whether a person is an athlete or not. The model will analyze data points and create a boundary that decides 
which side represents athletes and which side represents non-athletes. For simple models like logistic 
regression, the boundary is usually a straight line. For more complex models such as SVM with nonlinear 
kernels or neural networks, the boundary can become curved or irregular. The decision boundary is crucial 
because it directly affects how the model makes predictions; even small shifts in the boundary can change 
the classification of certain points. 
A-4. Explain dropout and how it helps in regularizing neural networks. 
Dropout is a regularization technique used in neural networks where randomly selected neurons are 
temporarily removed during each training iteration. This means that in every training step, some neurons do 
not participate in forward and backward propagation. By doing this, the network is forced to learn 
distributed representations rather than relying heavily on specific neurons. Dropout reduces the risk of 
overfitting by preventing the network from becoming too dependent on particular connections or features. 
When dropout is applied, the model becomes more robust because it learns to adapt even when part of the 
network is missing. During testing, no neurons are dropped, and instead, their outputs are scaled to maintain 
consistent predictions. Dropout is widely used in convolutional neural networks, fully connected layers, and 
sometimes in recurrent networks to improve generalization. 
A-5. What is tokenization in NLP? Mention two tokenization methods. 
Tokenization is the process of breaking text into smaller meaningful units called tokens. These tokens can be 
words, subwords, characters, or symbols depending on the model and task. Tokenization is essential because 
machine learning models cannot operate directly on raw text; they require numerical representations after 
splitting text into manageable components. For example, the sentence “Machine learning is powerful” can 
be split into individual words or smaller parts depending on the tokenizer. Two common tokenization 
methods are word-level tokenization and subword tokenization. In word-level tokenization, text is divided 
into separate words, making it simple but unable to handle unknown words. Subword tokenization methods 
like Byte Pair Encoding (BPE) and WordPiece break words into smaller pieces, allowing models to 
represent rare or new words more effectively.

B-2. Explain Dimensionality Reduction and compare PCA with Linear Discriminant 
Analysis (LDA). 
Dimensionality Reduction is the process of reducing the number of input variables while preserving 
important information. High-dimensional data often contains redundancy, noise, and correlations among 
features, which make learning more difficult. Dimensionality reduction improves computational efficiency, 
visualization, model accuracy, and generalization. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is an unsupervised 
technique that transforms data into new axes called principal components based on maximum variance. PCA 
does not use class labels; it only tries to capture the directions of highest variation. Linear Discriminant 
Analysis (LDA), on the other hand, is a supervised method that uses class labels and tries to find directions 
that maximize the separation between multiple classes. PCA is ideal for exploratory analysis, noise removal, 
and visualization, while LDA is more suited for classification tasks where maximizing class distinction is 
important. Both reduce dimensions, but PCA focuses on variance and LDA focuses on discrimination. 
B-3. Discuss Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Explain the roles of the generator 
and discriminator. 
Generative Adversarial Networks consist of two neural networks trained in opposition: a generator and a 
discriminator. The generator’s role is to create synthetic data that resembles the real dataset, such as images, 
audio, or text. It begins with random noise and learns to transform it into realistic samples. The 
discriminator’s role is to differentiate between real data and fake data produced by the generator. During 
training, the generator tries to fool the discriminator, while the discriminator tries to correctly classify real 
and fake samples. This competition is similar to a game where both networks improve continuously. Over 
time, the generator becomes skilled at producing highly realistic outputs, and the discriminator becomes 
better at detecting small differences. GANs are used in image synthesis, super-resolution, data 
augmentation, video generation, and style transfer. Their strength comes from the adversarial learning 
process that forces both networks to evolve. 
B-4. Explain the concept of Markov Decision Processes (MDP) in reinforcement learning and 
describe each component. 
A Markov Decision Process is a mathematical framework used to describe decision-making situations where 
outcomes are partly random and partly controlled by an agent. In reinforcement learning, an MDP provides 
the structure needed for an agent to learn optimal behavior through interactions with an environment. An 
MDP has several components. States represent the environment’s current situation. Actions are the choices 
available to the agent. Transition probabilities express the likelihood of moving from one state to another 
after taking an action. Rewards are numerical values received after taking actions, representing how good or 
bad the outcome is. The discount factor determines how much future rewards matter. Using these 
components, the agent tries to learn a policy that maximizes long-term cumulative rewards. MDPs form the 
foundation for algorithms like Q-learning and value iteration. 
B-5. Explain Time Series Forecasting using ML models such as ARIMA, LSTMs, and 
Prophet. 
Time Series Forecasting involves predicting future values based on historical data. ARIMA is a statistical 
model that uses autoregression, differencing, and moving average components to capture patterns like trend 
and autocorrelation. It works well for stationary data and requires careful parameter tuning using methods 
like AIC or PACF plots. LSTMs, a type of recurrent neural network, are capable of learning long-term 
dependencies and nonlinear relationships in time-series. They are useful when patterns are complex or when 
seasonality and sudden variations exist. Prophet is a forecasting model developed for simplicity and 
interpretability. It decomposes time series into trend, seasonality, and holidays, making it effective for 
business and daily forecasting tasks. All three models serve different scenarios: ARIMA for classical 
statistical forecasting, LSTMs for deep learning-based predictions, and Prophet for easy and robust 
modeling of seasonal patterns.

C-1. Define supervised learning with an example. 
• Supervised learning is a type of machine learning where the model is trained using labeled data. 
• Each training example contains both input features and a correct output label. 
• The goal is for the model to learn a mapping from inputs to outputs so it can predict labels for new data. 
• Classification and regression are the two main categories of supervised learning. 
• An example is training a model to identify handwritten digits using images labeled from 0 to 9. 
• The model learns patterns from the labeled images and can classify new handwritten digits accurately. 
C-2. What is precision in classification metrics? 
• Precision measures how many of the predicted positive samples are actually positive. 
• It is calculated as True Positives divided by the sum of True Positives and False Positives. 
• High precision means the model makes few false positive errors. 
• It is especially important in tasks like spam detection where wrong positives cause issues. 
• Precision helps evaluate the reliability of positive predictions. 
• It is often used along with recall to analyze classification performance. 
C-3. Explain the term kernel function. 
• A kernel function is a mathematical function used in algorithms like Support Vector Machines. 
• It helps transform data into a higher dimensional space without explicitly computing new features. 
• Kernels allow separating data that is not linearly separable in the original space. 
• Common kernels include linear kernel, polynomial kernel, and RBF kernel. 
• Kernel functions operate through inner products, making the computation efficient. 
• They enable models to learn complex boundaries and relationships. 
C-5. What is bagging? 
• Bagging stands for Bootstrap Aggregating, a method used to improve model stability. 
• It involves training multiple models on different bootstrapped samples of the training data. 
• These models’ predictions are then combined, usually through averaging or voting. 
• Bagging reduces variance and helps avoid overfitting, especially for unstable models. 
• Random Forest is a widely known example of bagging applied to decision trees. 
• The method increases robustness and generalization performance. 
C-6. Explain gradient vanishing. 
• Gradient vanishing is a problem where gradients become extremely small during backpropagation. 
• It causes early layers of deep networks to learn very slowly or stop learning. 
• This usually happens with activation functions like sigmoid or tanh. 
• As gradients shrink, weights fail to update effectively, delaying convergence. 
• Solutions include using ReLU activation, proper weight initialization, and residual networks. 
• It is one of the major challenges in training very deep neural models.
