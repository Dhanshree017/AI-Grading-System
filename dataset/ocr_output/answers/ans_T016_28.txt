A-1. What is data augmentation? Explain its importance in deep learning. 
Data augmentation is the process of artificially creating new data by applying transformations to 
existing data, such as rotating images or shuffling text. Its importance in deep learning lies 
in improving model generalization, reducing overfitting, and increasing robustness by 
exposing the model to a wider variety of realistic scenarios without collecting more data.  
How it works 
 Data augmentation works by applying realistic modifications to the original data to generate new, 
yet similar, data points. 
 For images, this can include: 
o Geometric transformations: Flipping, rotating, scaling, or cropping. 
o Color and brightness adjustments: Changing contrast, hue, or saturation. 
o Adding noise: Adding random noise to the image. 
 For text, common techniques include: 
o Synonym replacement 
o Sentence shuffling 
o Back-translation (translating text to another language and back) 
 For audio, methods like pitch shifting, time stretching, and noise injection are used.  
Why it is important in deep learning 
 Reduces overfitting: By creating diverse variations, augmentation prevents the model from 
memorizing the training data and helps it perform better on unseen data. 
 Improves generalization: Exposing the model to a wider range of data variations helps it learn 
the underlying patterns, making it better at handling different, real-world scenarios. 
 Enhances robustness: The model becomes more resilient to real-world variations like different 
lighting conditions, accents, or viewpoints. 
 Increases dataset size: It is a cost-effective way to increase the size of a dataset, especially 
when collecting and labeling new data is difficult or expensive.  
A-2. Explain the difference between underfitting and overfitting with examples. 
Underfitting and overfitting are two common problems encountered when training 
machine learning models, both of which hinder a model's ability to generalize to 
new, unseen data. 
Underfitting 
Underfitting occurs when a model is too simple to capture the underlying patterns in 
the training data. This results in the model performing poorly on both the training 
data and new, unseen data. It is characterized by high bias and low variance.  
Example: Imagine trying to predict house prices based on square footage using a 
simple linear regression model, but the actual relationship between square footage 
and price is non-linear (e.g., quadratic). The linear model would be too simplistic to 
capture this curved relationship, resulting in inaccurate predictions on both the 
training data and new houses. 
Overfitting 
Overfitting occurs when a model learns the training data too well, including its noise 
and irrelevant details. While the model performs exceptionally well on the training 
data, it fails to generalize to new, unseen data, leading to poor performance on test 
sets. It is characterized by low bias and high variance.  
Example: Consider training a highly complex polynomial regression model to predict 
house prices. If the model is too complex (e.g., a very high-degree polynomial), it 
might perfectly fit every data point in the training set, including any outliers or 
noise. However, when presented with a new house, this overly complex model might 
make wildly inaccurate predictions because it has essentially memorized the training 
data rather than learning the general trend. 
A-3. What is a decision boundary? Give an intuitive explanation with an example. 
Decision boundary is imaginary line the algorithm draws in its mind to separate things from 
other things. You cannot see it, but model thinks it exists like invisible fence for dogs. 
Imagine you have red balls and blue balls thrown randomly on ground, decision boundary is 
the invisible chalk line the model draws to divide them into two sides. But sometimes balls 
overlap and model panics and draw weird squiggly lines trying to separate them but still 
fails. It is the machine’s attempt to say, “everything on this side is category A and everything 
on that side is category B,” even if real world refuses to cooperate. Without decision 
boundary, machine would be completely confused like person trying to separate sugar and 
salt by smelling air from far away. 
A-5. What is tokenization in NLP? Mention two tokenization methods. 
Tokenization is like cutting a big sentence into tiny edible pieces for the NLP model because 
the model cannot swallow big sentence whole. So you break sentence into words, subwords, 
characters or whatever pieces you like. It is like breaking big biscuit into crumbs so baby can 
eat. NLP models love tokens. Two tokenization methods include word-based tokenization 
where each word becomes token and sub-word tokenization like Byte Pair Encoding where 
words get broken into small fragments because model cannot handle world’s vocabulary 
chaos. Without tokenization, the machine would stare at long sentences without knowing 
where one word ends and another begins.

B-1. Describe the Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm with its steps and 
applications. 
The EM algorithm is like a two-phase guessing game where machine keeps guessing 
unknown things then pretending its guess is correct and then again guessing with that fake 
correctness. It goes back and forth in loop until machine get tired or numbers stop changing. 
First step, expectation step, is where model calculates missing values based on its current 
wrong assumptions. Then in maximization step it updates its assumptions using those 
missing values it just made up. This continues until computer feels satisfied or stuck. 
Applications include clustering like Gaussian Mixture Models but also people sometimes 
claim it works for missing data, though sometimes EM also generate complete nonsense if 
used wrong because algorithm is sensitive like emotional person. EM always tries to 
maximize likelihood even if likelihood runs in direction of disaster. 
B-2. Explain Dimensionality Reduction and compare PCA with Linear Discriminant 
Analysis (LDA). 
Dimensionality reduction is basically trying to squeeze huge messy dataset into smaller 
number of features so models don’t explode while computing. PCA is method that finds 
directions of highest variance and rotates data along those axes like spinning globe until it 
finds nicest view. PCA totally ignore class labels because it only cares about maximizing 
variance like a person caring only about loud music. LDA however uses labels and tries to 
maximize separation between classes while minimizing within-class scatter. PCA is blind to 
class structure while LDA is obsessed with it. Dimensionality reduction helpful because 
without it, machine drown in ocean of meaningless dimensions and training gets slow and 
overfitting becomes monster. PCA works well when classes not important, LDA better when 
classification needed. 
B-3. Discuss Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Explain the roles of the generator 
and discriminator. 
GANs are like two neural networks in constant fight. Generator tries to produce fake images 
so good that discriminator gets fooled. Discriminator plays strict teacher who tries to detect 
which images are real and which are generator’s cheap fakes. Generator improves by 
learning how to fool discriminator, making more realistic fakes each round. Discriminator 
improves by getting better at catching those fakes. Over time, generator become powerful 
artist able to draw human-like faces, landscapes, objects etc, but sometimes produce creepy 
faces because GAN training unstable like toddler standing on one leg. These networks used 
in deepfake videos, image generation, photo restoration etc. The whole idea is adversarial 
training where both networks grow by fighting each other. 
B-5. Explain Time Series Forecasting using ML models such as ARIMA, LSTMs, and 
Prophet. 
Time series forecasting means predicting future values based on past behavior. ARIMA is 
classical statistical model using autoregression and moving averages with differencing to 
make series stationary. It works well when data has linear patterns and no long-term deep 
structure. LSTMs are neural networks designed to remember long sequences using memory 
cell, forget gate, input gate etc. They handle complex temporal relationships like long-term 
dependencies. Prophet is model developed by Facebook designed for business forecasting; it 
handles seasonality, holidays and trend changes easily with simple parameters. These models 
try to capture temporal patterns so future predictions look reasonable but each has strengths 
depending on nature of dataset. ARIMA good for simple linear structures, LSTM good for 
nonlinear deep patterns, Prophet good for business time series.

C-1. Define supervised learning with an example. 
Supervised learning is type of machine learning where model is given labeled data like 
images with names or numbers with answers. Model tries to learn mapping from inputs to 
outputs by adjusting weights. Example: giving thousand photos of dogs and cats labeled 
properly, model learns difference then predicts label for unseen photo. Supervised learning 
relies on teacher-like behavior from dataset. 
C-2. What is precision in classification metrics? 
Precision measures proportion of correct positive predictions among all positive predictions 
made by model. It is TP / (TP + FP). High precision means model makes fewer false 
positives. Useful when false positives costly like spam detection where marking legitimate 
email as spam is bad. 
C-3. Explain the term kernel function. 
A kernel function is a mathematical tool used in machine learning to transform data 
into a higher-dimensional space to make it easier to separate, without explicitly 
performing the transformation. It works by calculating the similarity between data 
points as if they were in this new, higher-dimensional space, allowing algorithms like 
support vector machines (SVMs) to handle non-linear data by implicitly working in 
that space. This is useful because it lets algorithms work with complex data patterns 
that are not linearly separable in their original form.  
How it works 
 Implicit transformation: Instead of computing the coordinates of every data point 
in a new, potentially very large, feature space, the kernel function directly calculates 
the result of the inner product of the data points in that space. 
 Measuring similarity: It essentially measures the similarity between two data points 
after they have been projected into a higher-dimensional space. 
C-4. Define feature scaling. 
Feature scaling is preprocessing technique to normalize or standardize features so all have 
comparable range and magnitude. Methods include min-max scaling and standardization. It 
helps algorithms like gradient descent and KNN work properly because features of different 
scales create imbalance. 
C-5. What is bagging? 
Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating) involves training multiple models on different bootstrap 
samples of dataset and combining their predictions. It reduces variance and improves 
stability especially for decision trees. Random Forest is example of bagging-based model. 
 Bootstrap Sampling: Multiple random subsets of the training data are created. 
Each subset is created by sampling from the original dataset with replacement, 
meaning some data points may appear multiple times and others may be left out. 
 Model Training: A separate model (like a decision tree) is trained on each of these 
bootstrap samples. 
 Prediction Aggregation: The predictions from all the individual models are 
combined to produce a final output. For regression tasks, this often involves 
averaging the predictions; for classification, it typically uses a majority vote.
