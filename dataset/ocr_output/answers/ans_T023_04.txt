A-1. Explain the role of validation sets in machine learning. Why is validation important, 
and how does it prevent model overfitting? 
A validation set is a separate part of the dataset used to tune model hyperparameters and 
evaluate performance during training. It helps measure how well the model generalizes 
before touching the test data. Validation is important because it prevents the model from 
memorizing training patterns by giving feedback on unseen samples. By comparing training 
and validation loss, we can identify overfitting, adjust regularization, choose parameters like 
learning rate, and stop training early to maintain generalization.

with suitable examples. 
Generative models are models that grow plants inside neural networks and let them choose 
their own categories. Discriminative models only calculate electricity signals and ignore the 
creativity of data. Generative models like GAN can clone mountains, while discriminative 
models like Naive Bayes tells difference between square clouds and round clouds. 
Generative models learn whole universe distribution, discriminative only compares objects

A-3. What is anomaly detection? Discuss its importance and common applications in real
world systems. 
Anomaly detection identifies observations that deviate from normal behavior. It is important 
because unusual events often signal system failures, threats, or fraud. Applications include 
fraud detection in transactions, intrusion detection in networks, detecting machine failure, 
spotting unusual medical patterns in health data, and identifying abnormal patterns in sensor
based industrial systems. 
A-4. Explain the concept of model interpretability. Why do certain industries require 
highly interpretable ML models? 
Model interpretability is the ability to understand why a model made a specific prediction. 
Some industries—like healthcare, finance, legal systems, and autonomous decision 
systems—require interpretability because decisions must be transparent, auditable, and 
compliant with regulations. Without interpretability, organizations cannot trust outputs, 
assess fairness, or justify predictions affecting safety or policy.

B-1. Explain the role of Data Preprocessing in building ML models. Discuss key steps 
such as data cleaning, encoding, transformation, and handling outliers. 
Data preprocessing is the crucial step of transforming raw, real-world data into a clean, 
structured, and usable format for machine learning algorithms. Its primary role is to 
ensure high-quality input, which directly impacts the performance, accuracy, efficiency, 
and reliability of the final model. Without it, models may struggle to identify meaningful 
patterns, leading to biased or inaccurate predictions (the "garbage in, garbage out" 
principle).  
Key Steps in Data Preprocessing 
The main tasks involved in data preprocessing for building ML models include: 
 Data Cleaning 
This process identifies and corrects errors, inconsistencies, and noise in the dataset to ensure 
a reliable foundation for modeling. 
o Handling Missing Values: Gaps in data are a common issue. Techniques include deletion 
(removing rows or columns if the missing data percentage is small) or imputation (filling the 
missing values with statistical measures like the mean, median, or mode for numerical data, 
or the most frequent category for categorical data). 
o Removing Duplicates: Redundant records can skew the model by overemphasizing certain 
patterns. Identifying and removing these ensures the model learns from unique data points. 
o Correcting Inconsistencies: This involves standardizing formats, such as ensuring 
consistent date formats (e.g., MM/DD/YYYY) or uniform labels for the same category (e.g., 
"Male" vs. "M"). 
 Encoding 
Most machine learning algorithms require numerical input and cannot directly process text or 
categorical variables (data that represents groups or categories). 
o Label Encoding: Assigns a unique integer to each category. This is suitable only for ordinal 
data where the categories have a meaningful rank or order (e.g., 'Low' to 'High'). 
o One-Hot Encoding: Creates new binary (0 or 1) columns for each category. This is the 
standard method for nominal data (categories without an intrinsic order, like colors or city 
names) to prevent the model from assuming an artificial order. 
 Transformation 
This involves converting data into a format suitable for the specific requirements of the ML 
algorithm. 
o Feature Scaling: Ensures features with different units or magnitudes (e.g., age vs. income) 
have a similar scale so that no single feature dominates the model training. 
o Standardization (Z-score normalization): Rescales data to have a mean of 0 and a standard 
deviation of 1. It is often a safe default and less sensitive to outliers. 
o Normalization (Min-Max scaling): Rescales features to a fixed range, typically between 0 
and 1. It is useful for algorithms like neural networks that expect input within a specific 
range. 
o Other Transformations: May include mathematical transformations (like log 
transformation for skewed data), or creating new features from existing ones (feature 
engineering) to enhance the data's predictive power. 
 Handling Outliers 
Outliers are data points that significantly deviate from other observations and can skew 
statistical analyses and model predictions. 
o Detection: Outliers are typically identified using visualization methods like box plots or 
statistical techniques such as the Z-score or Interquartile Range (IQR) method. 
o Management: Depending on the context, outliers can be removed (if they are errors), 
capped (limiting their value to a certain threshold), or the data can be transformed to reduce 
their impact. It is important to use domain knowledge to determine if an outlier is a genuine 
anomaly (which might be important for tasks like fraud detection) or a data entry error.

C-1. Discuss the challenges of deploying ML models in real-world environments. Explain 
issues like data drift, model monitoring, scalability, hardware constraints, security 
risks, and continuous retraining . 
Deploying ML models brings several challenges. Data drift occurs when input patterns 
change over time, causing performance degradation. Model monitoring ensures the system 
continuously checks predictions, errors, latency, and data quality. Scalability concerns arise 
when user load increases and the system must handle high traffic. Hardware constraints 
affect models deployed on mobile and embedded systems with limited resources. Security 
risks include adversarial attacks, model theft, and data poisoning. Continuous retraining is 
needed to keep the model updated with current data patterns and maintain accuracy over 
time. 
C-2. Explain in detail how machine learning is used in healthcare. Describe applications 
(diagnosis, prediction, medical imaging, drug discovery), challenges (data quality, 
privacy, bias), and the steps involved in developing an ML-based healthcare system. 
Machine learning (ML) revolutionizes healthcare by analyzing complex data for faster 
diagnosis, personalized treatment, predictive risk assessment, enhanced medical imaging 
analysis (X-rays, MRIs), and accelerated drug discovery, but faces hurdles like ensuring data 
quality, upholding privacy (HIPAA), tackling algorithmic bias, and integrating into 
workflows, with development involving data prep, model training, validation, and 
continuous monitoring for improved outcomes and operational efficiency.  
Applications in Healthcare 
 Diagnosis & Prediction: Detects diseases (e.g., diabetes, cancer) earlier by spotting patterns 
in patient records, genetic data, and vitals; predicts disease progression and patient risk for 
adverse events. 
 Medical Imaging: Uses Deep Learning (CNNs) to analyze X-rays, MRIs, CT scans for 
anomalies (tumors, lesions) with high accuracy, assisting radiologists. 
 Drug Discovery: Speeds up finding new drugs by analyzing molecular interactions, 
predicting compound safety/efficacy, modeling drug structures, and optimizing clinical trials. 
 Personalized Medicine: Tailors treatments using individual data (genetics, lifestyle) to 
predict best therapeutic responses. 
 Clinical Operations: Improves efficiency, manages fraud, automates tasks, and supports 
research.  
Key Challenges 
 Data Quality & Quantity: Requires large, clean, labeled datasets, which are often 
fragmented or messy. 
 Data Privacy & Security: Handling sensitive patient data (PHI) necessitates strict 
compliance (e.g., HIPAA). 
 Bias & Fairness: Models can perpetuate or amplify biases present in training data, leading 
to unfair outcomes for certain demographics. 
 Interpretability (Explainability): "Black box" models make it hard to understand why a 
decision was made, crucial for clinical trust. 
 Integration & Workflow: Seamlessly embedding ML tools into existing hospital systems 
and clinical workflows.  
Development Steps for an ML Healthcare System 
1. Define Problem: Clearly state the clinical need (e.g., detect pneumonia in X-rays). 
2. Data Collection & Prep: Gather, clean, label (by experts), and preprocess diverse data 
(images, EHRs). 
3. Feature Engineering: Select/create relevant data features (e.g., lesion shape, vital sign 
trends). 
4. Model Selection: Choose algorithm (Supervised Learning for labeled data like imaging, 
RNNs for time-series). 
5. Training & Validation: Train model on training data, tune parameters, validate on hold-out 
sets. 
6. Evaluation: Test on unseen data using metrics like sensitivity, specificity, AUC, ensuring 
clinical relevance. 
7. Deployment & Integration: Integrate into clinical systems (EHRs, PACS). 
8. Monitoring & Maintenance: Continuously monitor performance, retrain as needed for data 
drift.
