A-a. Explain the concept of model robustness in machine learning. Discuss why 
robustness is important and how it can be improved in learning systems. 
Robustness is basically "common sense" for a computer. If a model is robust, it doesn't freak 
out if the data is a little bit messy. 
 Why it's important: Think about a face-unlock feature on a phone. If you wear 
glasses or have a pimple, the phone should still recognize you. If it wasn't robust, 
you'd be locked out of your phone every time you changed your hair. 
 How to improve it: You have to feed it "bad" examples. You show the computer 
pictures of yourself in the dark, in the rain, or wearing a hat. By showing it these 
"noisy" versions, the computer learns to focus on your actual face, not the background. 
A-b. Describe the role of data representation in machine learning. Explain how different 
ways of representing data can affect learning outcomes. 
Data representation is just the way you translate the real world into something a computer 
can see. 
 The Effect: Imagine trying to teach someone what a "car" is. If you only show them a 
list of every single bolt and screw (bad representation), they will be confused. But if 
you show them a picture (good representation), they get it instantly. 
 Outcome: If you give a machine the wrong "view" of the data, it will fail. If you want 
to predict the weather, telling the computer the "color of the sky" is okay, but telling it 
the "air pressure" is much better. Pick the wrong clues, and you get the wrong 
answers. 
A-e. Describe the responsibilities of an ML engineer after model deployment. Explain 
tasks related to monitoring, updating, documentation, and ethical compliance. 
The job doesn't end when the "Go" button is pressed. It's like being a doctor for the AI. 
 Monitoring: You have to check the AI’s "heartbeat" every day. Is it still getting 
answers right, or is it getting "sick" and making mistakes? 
 Updating: If the world changes (like a new slang word appearing), you have to give 
the AI a "booster shot" of new data. 
 Documentation: You have to write a diary of everything you did so that if you quit, 
the next person knows how to fix the AI. 
 Ethical Compliance: You have to make sure the AI isn't being a "bully" or being 
unfair to certain people based on their race or gender.

B-e. What is a machine learning algorithm? The "logic engine." It’s the set of math steps the 
computer follows to turn your messy data into a smart guess. 
B-f. Define overfitting. When the AI tries too hard. It memorizes the exact data so perfectly 
that it can't handle anything even slightly different. 
B-g. What is data preprocessing? The "cleanup." You’re scrubbing the data to get rid of 
mistakes, empty spots, and weird formatting before the AI sees it. 
B-h. What is a prediction in machine learning? The "final guess." It's the AI saying, "Based 
on what I've seen, I think the answer is X." 
B-i. Define model evaluation. The "report card." You test the AI with a secret set of questions 
to see if it actually knows its stuff or if it's just guessing. 
B-j. What is learning from experience in ML? It means the more data (experience) the 
computer gets, the fewer mistakes it makes. It literally gets smarter over time.
