A-a. Explain the concept of learning bias in machine learning. Discuss different sources of bias and their 
impact on model generalization. 
Learning bias is when the computer is lazy and only learns the simple stuff. It's like having a favorite food 
and ignoring all the other food. 
 Concept: Bias is the error from the model being too stupid. It makes too many assumptions, like 
assuming everything is a straight line when it's not. 
 Sources of Bias: 
1. Too Simple Model: If you use a basic model (like a one-layer model) for a very complicated 
problem (like recognizing faces), it has high bias. 
2. Bad Training Data: If you only show the model pictures of cats and then ask it to find a dog, 
that's a bias problem in the data. 
 Impact on Generalization: High bias causes underfitting. The model fails badly on the training 
data, so of course it will fail on the new test data too. It just misses the whole point of the problem. 
A-b. Describe the role of assumptions in machine learning algorithms. Explain how assumptions 
influence model selection and performance. 
Assumptions are just things we hope are true about the data. We have to make them, or the computer can't 
even start. 
 Role of Assumptions: They help the computer decide what kind of model to use. If we assume the 
data is simple, we pick a simple model. 
 How Assumptions Influence Selection: 
o If we assume everything is in a line, we use a model called Linear Regression. That's a good 
assumption because those models are fast. 
o If we assume things are very complicated and non-linear, we use a big model like a Neural 
Network, but then it gets slow and hungry for power. 
 Performance Influence: If your assumption is wrong (e.g., you assume a straight line when the data 
is a curve), the model will be high bias and perform terribly. If you don't assume anything, the model 
is too flexible and ends up just memorizing the training data, which is also bad performance. 
A-c. Explain the concept of generalization in machine learning. Discuss why a model that performs well 
on training data may fail on unseen data. 
Generalization is about making sure the model is not just a one-trick pony. 
 Concept: Generalization is the ability of the model to work well on new data it has never seen 
before. If the training score is high, the generalization score should also be high. 
 Why Failure on Unseen Data Occurs: This is the classic problem of overfitting. 
1. Memorizing Noise: The model is too complicated and learns the tiny mistakes and noise in 
the training data, instead of the main pattern. It's like a student memorizing the textbook 
word-for-word but not understanding the concepts. 
2. Too Small Training Set: If the training data is too small, the model doesn't see enough 
variety, so when the new test data comes along, it's surprised and doesn't know what to do. 
3. Data Change: When the model is deployed, the real-world data starts changing (this is called 
Concept Drift), making the model's old rules incorrect for the new reality. 
A-d. Discuss the importance of data quality in machine learning systems. Explain how noise, missing 
values, and inconsistencies affect learning outcomes. 
Data quality is crucial in machine learning (ML) because "garbage in, garbage out" applies: poor data (noise, 
missing values, inconsistencies) leads to inaccurate, biased, and unreliable models, causing poor 
performance, biased decisions, and wasted resources, whereas clean, consistent data enables accurate pattern 
recognition, better generalization, and fair outcomes, making data quality foundational for effective AI. 
Noise (irrelevant data), missing values (incomplete records), and inconsistencies (format/logic errors) distort 
patterns, leading to higher error rates, biased predictions (especially for minority classes), and models that 
fail to generalize, requiring extensive cleaning and validation for fairness and accuracy.  
Importance of Data Quality 
 Accuracy & Reliability: High-quality data helps models learn true patterns, leading to better predictions in 
real-world scenarios. 
 Efficiency: Clean data reduces time-consuming preprocessing, speeding up model development and 
deployment. 
 Fairness & Ethics: Addresses biases in data, preventing discriminatory outcomes and ensuring models are 
equitable, especially in sensitive areas like healthcare or justice. 
 Trust & Explainability: Well-documented, clean data builds trust and helps stakeholders understand model 
decisions.  
How Data Issues Affect Learning Outcomes 
 Noise (Irrelevant/Erroneous Data): 
o Impact: Distorts the true signal, confusing the model and reducing its ability to find meaningful patterns, 
leading to lower accuracy and poor generalization. 
o Example: Random errors in customer ratings causing a recommendation system to suggest irrelevant 
products. 
 Missing Values: 
o Impact: Creates gaps in understanding, forcing models to rely on incomplete information, potentially 
leading to skewed results or needing imputation methods (like mean/median) that might introduce bias. 
o Example: Many missing age entries in a dataset skewing age-based predictions. 
 Inconsistencies & Bias: 
o Impact: Inconsistent formats (e.g., "USA" vs. "United States") or biased labels (unfairly applied labels) 
teach the model incorrect relationships, amplifying systemic inequalities and resulting in biased, unfair, or 
discriminatory decisions. 
o Example: A loan application model trained on historically biased data might unfairly deny loans to certain 
demographics. 
 Data Imbalance: 
o Impact: Models favor majority classes, performing poorly on underrepresented (minority) classes, leading 
to biased predictions and ineffective solutions for those groups. 
o Example: A fraud detection model trained mostly on non-fraudulent transactions might miss most actual 
fraud cases.  
By proactively managing these data quality issues through cleaning, imputation, bias mitigation, and 
continuous monitoring, developers can build robust, accurate, and fair machine learning systems.  
A-e. Explain the difference between shallow learning and deep learning approaches. Discuss their 
characteristics, strengths, and limitations in theory. 
This is about the old models versus the new, big models. 
 Shallow Learning (SL): These are the old, simple models. 
o Characteristics: They have only a few layers. You have to manually do all the hard work of 
feature engineering (telling the computer which columns are important). 
o Strengths: They are easy to understand (transparent) and work okay with small amounts of 
data. 
o Limitations: They can't solve really complicated problems like image recognition. 
 Deep Learning (DL): These are the new, fancy models. 
o Characteristics: They have lots and lots of layers. The model figures out all the features 
automatically (like recognizing edges, then eyes, then a face). 
o Strengths: They are the best for complex problems like vision and language. 
o Limitations: They are black boxes (we can't see how they work), they need huge amounts of 
data, and they need expensive computers (like GPUs) to train.
