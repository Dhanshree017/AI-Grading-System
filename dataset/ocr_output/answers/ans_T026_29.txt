A-b. Describe the role of assumptions in machine learning algorithms. Explain how assumptions 
influence model selection and performance. 
Definition of Assumptions in Machine Learning 
Assumptions are predefined beliefs about data distribution, relationships, or patterns that guide how a 
machine learning algorithm learns from data. 
Role and Influence of Assumptions 
1. Simplifies Learning Process – Assumptions reduce complexity of learning. 
2. Linear Assumptions – Linear models assume a linear relationship between features and output. 
3. Independence Assumption – Naive Bayes assumes features are independent. 
4. Data Distribution Assumption – Some models assume normal distribution of data. 
5. Influence on Model Choice – Incorrect assumptions lead to wrong model selection. 
6. Bias Introduction – Strong assumptions increase bias in the model. 
7. Performance Impact – Models fail if assumptions do not match real data. 
8. Generalization Effect – Proper assumptions improve generalization. 
9. Flexibility vs Simplicity – Complex models make fewer assumptions but risk overfitting. 
10. Conclusion – Understanding assumptions is essential for effective ML performance. 
A-c. Explain the concept of generalization in machine learning. Discuss why a model that performs well 
on training data may fail on unseen data. 
Definition of Generalization 
Generalization refers to the ability of a machine learning model to perform well on new, unseen data after 
being trained on a training dataset. 
Explanation of Generalization Failure 
1. Overfitting – Model memorizes training data instead of learning patterns. 
2. High Model Complexity – Excessively complex models capture noise. 
3. Insufficient Training Data – Limited data leads to poor pattern learning. 
4. Data Leakage – Training data contains information from test data. 
5. Poor Feature Selection – Irrelevant features harm generalization. 
6. Noise in Data – Noisy data misguides learning. 
7. Bias–Variance Tradeoff – High variance reduces generalization ability. 
8. Distribution Shift – Training and test data distributions differ. 
9. Lack of Regularization – No penalty for complexity increases overfitting.
