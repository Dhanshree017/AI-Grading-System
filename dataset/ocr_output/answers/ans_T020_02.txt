A-a) Explain how data drift and concept drift affect the long-term performance of machine learning 
models. Discuss the different types of drift and practical strategies to detect and handle them. 
Short idea: 
Models assume future look like past. When that stop, model fail. Two main ways: data drift (inputs change) 
and concept drift (relationship input→output change). 
Types (simple): 
 Covariate drift: features change distribution (example: users become older). 
 Label prior drift: class frequencies change (example: more fraud events in holiday). 
 Concept drift: mapping P(Y|X) change — rules of world changed (example: new fraud tactic). 
 Sudden / incremental / recurring: sudden = quick flip, incremental = slow slide, recurring = 
seasonal come-back. 
How they hurt long-term performance: 
 Model loses calibration, predictions biased, more errors, business loss. 
 Validation scores become stale; model confident but wrong. 
Detect strategies: 
 Stat tests for X: KS-test, PSI, monitoring feature histograms. 
 Performance monitoring: watch rolling metrics (precision, recall) on recent labeled samples. 
 Unsupervised detection: anomaly detectors on feature vectors, change point detection. 
 Shadow models / champion-challenger: compare new model outputs to baseline. 
Handle strategies (practical): 
 Retrain schedule: periodic retrain with fresh data. 
 Trigger retrain: retrain when drift metric or performance fall crosses threshold. 
 Online / incremental learning: adapt continuously with streaming updates. 
 Ensembling & weighting: keep old model + new model and blend to smooth changes. 
 Feature hardening: remove fragile features, use features less likely to shift. 
 Data pipelines & monitoring: log inputs, create labeling loop for quick ground truth. 
Practical note: keep human-in-loop for root cause before blind retrain; retrain can amplify bad labels if not 
careful. 
A-b) Discuss the role of embedding techniques in machine learning. Explain how text, images, or 
categorical data can be transformed into meaningful representations for models. 
Short idea: 
Embeddings convert complex symbolic or high-dim inputs into dense numeric vectors that models easily 
use. Embeddings capture similarity and semantics. 
Text embeddings: 
 Word vectors: Word2Vec/GloVe map words to vectors so similar words close. 
 Contextual vectors: BERT/GPT produce vectors that depend on sentence context. 
 Sentence/document embeddings: average or specialized encoders (SBERT) make whole-sentence 
vectors. 
Image embeddings: 
 Feed image through pretrained CNN or ViT, take activation layer as feature vector. 
 These vectors capture shape, texture, semantics. Used for similarity, retrieval, downstream tasks. 
Categorical embeddings: 
 Instead of large one-hot, learn small dense vector per category (trainable embedding layer). 
 Good for high-cardinality IDs (user id, product id), let model learn relations between categories. 
Why embeddings help: 
 Reduce sparsity, reduce dimension, capture latent similarity. 
 Allow transfer learning: reuse embedding from big model to small task. 
 Support multi-modal fusion: combine image+text embeddings in single model. 
How to build/use: 
 Pretrain on big corpus then fine-tune. 
 Freeze embedding if small dataset, or fine-tune when domain-shift exists. 
 Normalize embeddings, use cosine similarity for retrieval tasks. 
Practical caution: embeddings can capture bias from training data; monitor and sanitize. 
A-c) Describe the challenges associated with training models on imbalanced datasets. Explain advanced 
techniques such as cost-sensitive learning, synthetic sample generation, and threshold adjustment. 
Short idea: 
Imbalance means minority important class under-represented. Naive training biased to majority; metrics 
misleading. 
Main challenges: 
 Model predict majority always → high accuracy but useless. 
 Minority recall low → real harms (fraud, disease). 
 Optimization dominated by frequent class gradients. 
 Risk of overfitting when oversampling naively. 
Advanced techniques: 
1. Cost-sensitive learning 
 Assign higher loss weight for minority errors (weighted cross-entropy). 
 Use class-weighting or custom loss that penalize false negatives more. 
 Works at model-level, no data change required. 
2. Synthetic sample generation 
 SMOTE: create synthetic minority by interpolating between neighbors. 
 ADASYN / Borderline-SMOTE: focus synthesis on harder regions near boundary. 
 Generative models: use GANs or VAEs to produce realistic minority examples. 
 Careful: synthetic must be realistic, else hurt generalization. 
3. Threshold adjustment 
 Instead of default 0.5, move decision threshold to favor minority detection. 
 Use Precision-Recall curve or cost function to pick threshold matching business trade-off. 
4. Ensemble & bagging strategies 
 Balanced bagging: sample balanced mini-batches for each tree. 
 One-class classifiers or anomaly detection for extreme imbalance. 
5. Evaluation metrics & calibration 
 Use PR-AUC, F1, recall@k instead of accuracy. 
 Calibrate probabilities (Platt scaling, isotonic) before threshold tuning. 
Practical tips: combine methods: weighting + realistic oversampling + threshold tuning, and validate on 
stratified holdout to avoid optimistic bias. 
A-d) Explain the concept of model monitoring in ML systems. What parameters are tracked, and why is 
continuous monitoring essential for deployed models? 
Short idea: 
Model monitoring = ongoing observation of model health and input environment to detect failures, drift, and 
performance issues. 
Parameters to track: 
Data quality & input monitoring 
 Missing/NaN counts, invalid formats. 
 Feature distribution stats (mean, std, min/max). 
 Schema changes (new categories). 
Drift & distribution 
 Population Stability Index (PSI), KS distance, embedding similarity. 
 Covariate and target distribution changes. 
Prediction & performance 
 Model metrics on recent labeled data: accuracy, recall, precision, ROC-AUC, RMSE. 
 Prediction distribution: score histograms, predicted class balance. 
Operational metrics 
 Latency, qps (queries per second), error rates, resource usage. 
 Queue length, timeouts, rate-limiting events. 
Business KPIs & fairness 
 Downstream business metrics (conversion, churn). 
 Group-level metrics for fairness: per-group error rates. 
Why continuous monitoring essential: 
 Real world changes cause silent failures (data drift, bad input) not visible until big harm. 
 Early detection prevents customer impact and legal/regulatory issues. 
 Helps decide retrain frequency, rollback or trigger human review. 
 Enables root-cause debugging: linking metric drop with input changes. 
Practical set-up: alerting thresholds, dashboards, automated periodic sampling for labeling, and runbook 
for actions. 
A-e) Discuss the difference between batch learning and online learning. Explain the scenarios where 
online learning is preferred and the challenges involved. 
Batch learning (offline): 
 Train on whole dataset at once, often in multiple epochs. 
 Good when data static or periodic retrain acceptable. 
 Easier to tune and reproduce. 
Online learning (streaming/incremental): 
 Model updates continuously with each new sample or small batches. 
 Useful for streaming data, low-latency adaptation, limited memory. 
When online preferred: 
 High-velocity domains: fraud detection, clickstream, sensor telemetry. 
 Non-stationary environments: concept drift quickly affecting performance. 
 Memory or compute constraints: cannot store whole history. 
Challenges in online learning: 
 Catastrophic forgetting: new data can erase earlier patterns. Need replay buffers or regularization. 
 Noisy labels: incremental updates propagate label noise quickly. 
 Hyperparameter tuning: learning rates, decay schedules need care. 
 Evaluation difficulty: need streaming evaluation metrics and holdout strategies. 
 Stability vs plasticity trade-off: balance adaptivity with stability (use elastic weight consolidation, 
weighted updates). 
Practical strategies: use online learning for quick adaptation, but keep periodic batch retrain for stability; 
hybrid approaches combine streaming updates with periodic full retrain.

B-a) What is feature leakage (data leakage) in machine learning? Explain its causes and how it impacts 
model performance. 
Definition & effect: 
Feature leakage occurs when model training inadvertently uses information that would not be available at 
prediction time, especially features correlated with target because of future events or preprocessing that 
peeked into labels. This produces inflated performance in training/validation, but fails in production. 
Causes (common): 
 Temporal leakage: using future data (e.g., including next-day sales when predicting today's 
demand). 
 Target leakage: features computed using target (e.g., “total_claim_amount” when predicting 
whether a claim will be fraud). 
 Improper preprocessing: applying normalization/scalers to full dataset before split. 
 Cross-contamination: same entity records in train and test (e.g., same customer across splits). 
 Feature engineering bug: using aggregate stats computed on whole dataset rather than only training 
partition. 
Impact on performance: 
 Over-optimistic validation metrics; model looks great offline but fails online. 
 Production breakdowns when leaked signals absent. 
 Misdiagnosis: time wasted tuning model instead fixing leakage. 
Detection & prevention: 
 Temporal split & group split: use time-aware or group-aware splitting. 
 Pipeline discipline: apply preprocessing inside train fold only (fit on train, transform on test). 
 Feature provenance: track how each feature created; flag any feature using future info. 
 Shadow test: create realistic production-like test where leaked features are removed; see drop. 
 Domain expert review: get domain team to check if feature available at inference time. 
 Automated checks: feature importance sudden spike may indicate leakage; high-correlating features 
with target should be scrutinized. 
Mitigation: remove leaking feature, rebuild pipeline, re-evaluate; if leak from label noise, fix labeling 
process. 
B-b) Explain the role of evaluation pipelines in ML workflows. Why is it important to separate 
validation, testing, and deployment evaluation? 
Role of evaluation pipelines: 
Evaluation pipeline is the standardized process that takes model outputs and computes metrics, runs 
diagnostics, and produces reports. It ensures reproducibility, fair comparison, and automated gating for 
deployment. 
Components of a robust evaluation pipeline: 
 Consistent preprocessing applied to all data splits. 
 Metric computation with clear definitions. 
 Cross-validation or holdout strategies automated. 
 Error analysis tooling (confusion matrices, residual plots). 
 Robustness checks (adversarial, out-of-distribution tests). 
 Fairness/ethical checks per subgroup. 
 Production-like tests (latency, resource usage). 
Why separate validation, testing, and deployment evaluation: 
Validation (model selection) 
 Used during development to tune hyperparameters and choose architecture. 
 If we use test set repeatedly for tuning, it leaks info and becomes biased. 
Test (final estimate) 
 Held-out set used once to estimate generalization after all tuning is complete. 
 Provides unbiased performance estimate for reporting and comparison. 
Deployment evaluation (monitoring) 
 Continuous evaluation on live data and business KPIs after model is deployed. 
 Measures real-world impact, drift, latency, and fairness in production contexts. 
Importance of separation: 
 Prevents data leakage and overfitting to test set. 
 Ensures honest performance reporting and regulatory compliance. 
 Deployment evaluation captures operational realities not present in offline test (input distribution 
changes, user behavior, feedback loops). 
Practical advice: automate pipeline, store hashes of datasets, log experiments, and have gating rules for 
promotion to production. 
B-c) Describe how transfer learning improves model development. Mention scenarios where transfer 
learning is especially useful. 
Essence of transfer learning: 
Transfer learning reuses a model (or part of it) trained on one large dataset/task to help training on another 
related task with limited data. It saves compute, improves performance and speeds convergence. 
Why it improves development: 
 Learned representations: early layers capture generic features (edges, syntax) transferrable across 
tasks. 
 Reduced data need: fine-tune with smaller labeled set. 
 Faster training & compute saving: fewer epochs and smaller learning-rate needed. 
 Better generalization: pretraining on diverse data regularizes model. 
Common approaches: 
 Feature extraction: freeze pretrained layers and train new head for target task. 
 Fine-tuning: unfreeze some top layers and train jointly with lower learning rate. 
 Adapter modules / LoRA: add small trainable adapters to large pre-trained models to adapt without 
full fine-tune. 
Scenarios especially useful: 
 Small labeled datasets: medical imaging, rare languages. 
 Domain with high labeling cost: satellite imagery, diagnostics. 
 Rapid prototyping: use pretrained NLP/image models to iterate quickly. 
 Resource constraints: leverage public pretrained weights rather than training from scratch. 
Caveats: check domain mismatch; sometimes pretraining domain too different (e.g., photos vs medical 
scans) so adaptation required (domain-adaptive pretraining). 
B-d) What is the purpose of hyperparameter search strategies like Grid Search and Random Search? 
Compare them briefly. 
Purpose: 
Hyperparameters control model behavior (regularization, tree depth, learning rate). Good hyperparameters 
can significantly boost performance. Search strategies aim to find near-optimal hyperparameter settings 
automatically. 
Grid Search: 
 Enumerates all combinations over a specified discrete grid. 
 Guarantees coverage of chosen grid. 
 Works if search space small and you expect important interactions across parameters. 
 Very expensive for high-dimensional or continuous spaces. 
Random Search: 
 Samples random combinations from distributions for each hyperparameter. 
 More efficient in high-dimensional spaces because often only a few hyperparameters matter strongly; 
random search explores more unique settings in fixed budget. 
 Empirically often finds good settings faster than grid. 
Other advanced methods: 
 Bayesian optimization: model-based search (e.g., Gaussian Process) to propose next point based on 
past results. 
 Hyperband / Successive halving: allocate resource adaptively, prune bad configs early. 
 Population-based training: evolve hyperparams during training. 
Comparison summary: 
 Grid: exhaustive, simple, expensive. 
 Random: simple, often efficient, better coverage in high-dim. 
 Bayesian/Meta: best for constrained budgets, more complex to implement. 
Practical tip: combine random search (coarse) then refine with Bayesian; use early stopping to save 
compute.
