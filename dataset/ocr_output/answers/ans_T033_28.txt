A-1. 
Explain the role of assumptions in machine learning models and analyze how incorrect 
assumptions can affect model behavior and decision-making.  
In the realm of machine learning, no algorithm can learn without a set of prior beliefs or constraints, 
scientifically known as Inductive Bias. These assumptions are the mathematical "shortcuts" that allow a 
model to generalize from a finite training set to an infinite number of unseen instances.  
• The Structural Role of Assumptions:  
o Hypothesis Space Definition: Assumptions define the boundaries of the Hypothesis Space 
($H$). For instance, a Linear Regression model assumes that the relationship between the 
independent variables ($X$) and the dependent variable ($y$) is linear. This narrows the 
search from every possible mathematical function to only linear ones.  
o Feature Independence: The Naive Bayes algorithm assumes "conditional independence" 
between features. While rarely true in reality, this assumption simplifies the computation of 
joint probabilities, allowing the model to perform surprisingly well on high-dimensional data 
like text.  
o Smoothness and Locality: Many algorithms, like K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN), assume that 
points close to each other in the feature space are likely to share the same label.  
• Analysis of Incorrect Assumptions (Model Misspecification): o  Systemic Bias: If the "True 
Function" that governs the data is non-linear (e.g., a complex curve) and we apply a linear 
assumption, the model will suffer from High Bias. No matter how much data you provide, the model 
will remain "underfitted" because its fundamental logic is too rigid to capture the reality.  
o Decision-Making Failures: In real-world applications, incorrect assumptions lead to 
catastrophic failures. In Algorithmic Trading, if a model assumes market volatility follows a 
"Normal Distribution" (ignoring "Fat Tails" or extreme events), it will severely underestimate 
the risk of a market crash, leading to massive financial loss.  
o Bias and Unfairness: If a hiring algorithm assumes that "years of experience" is the only 
valid predictor of success, it might unfairly penalize younger, highly skilled candidates or 
those who took career breaks, leading to skewed corporate demographics.  
A-2. 
Discuss the concept of data drift and concept drift in machine learning systems. Explain their 
impact on long-term model performance.  
Machine learning models operate under the Stationarity Assumption—the idea that the future will look 
like the past. However, in production environments, the statistical properties of data evolve over time, 
leading to "Model Decay."  
• Data Drift (Covariate Shift):  
Data drift occurs when the distribution of the input features ($P(X)$) changes, even though the 
relationship between $X$ and the target $y$ remains constant.  
o Example: A health-risk model trained on data from rural populations will experience data 
drift if it is suddenly deployed in a high-density urban city. The "types" of people (inputs) 
have changed, even if the biological rules of health remain the same.   Concept Drift 
(Posterior Probability Shift):  
Concept drift is more critical; it occurs when the fundamental relationship between the input and the 
target ($P(y|X)$) changes.  
o Example: In Spam Detection, what was considered a "safe email" in 2010 (concept A) 
might be a "phishing attack" in 2025 (concept B). The "concept" of spam has evolved.  
Long-term Impact on Performance:  
o Silent Failure: Drift is dangerous because it doesn't cause a "crash." The model continues to 
output predictions, but its Precision and Recall gradually sink.  
o Loss of Reliability: For long-term systems like Self-Driving Cars or Credit Scoring, drift 
means the model becomes a liability.  
o The Solution: Long-term performance requires MLOps strategies, including automated data 
monitoring, "Champion-Challenger" testing, and scheduled retraining pipelines to align the 
model with the current "data reality."
