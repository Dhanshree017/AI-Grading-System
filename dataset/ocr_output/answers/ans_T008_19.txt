1-a) Write about Inductive Bias 
Inductive bias refers to the assumptions or prior knowledge that a learning algorithm 
uses to predict outcomes for unseen instances. It is a crucial element that guides the 
model to select one hypothesis over another when multiple hypotheses are consistent 
with the training data. By introducing inductive bias, models can generalize 
effectively from limited data and avoid random or arbitrary predictions. 
1. Directs the model to prefer certain hypotheses. 
2. Reduces ambiguity in hypothesis selection. 
3. Enables better generalization on unseen data. 
4. Can be explicit (rules) or implicit (algorithm design). 
5. Strong bias may limit flexibility. 
6. Weak bias may result in overfitting. 
7. Influences model performance and accuracy. 
8. Essential for efficient learning with limited data. 
9. Forms the foundation for knowledge-based AI systems. 
1-b) Define Hypothesis 
A hypothesis is a candidate function or model that describes the mapping from 
inputs to outputs in a learning problem. It represents a possible explanation for the 
observed data and is evaluated based on its ability to predict unseen instances 
accurately. Selecting the appropriate hypothesis is essential for effective learning and 
generalization. 
1. Represents a possible solution to a learning problem. 
2. Maps input features to output predictions. 
3. Multiple hypotheses can fit the same dataset. 
4. Evaluated using training and test datasets. 
5. Supports the model’s ability to generalize. 
6. Can vary in complexity based on model design. 
7. Hypotheses can be refined iteratively. 
8. Affects prediction accuracy and performance. 
9. Serves as the foundation for predictive modeling. 
1-c) Write about Sampling Theories 
Sampling theory is the study of selecting a representative subset from a larger 
population to make inferences about the whole population. Proper sampling ensures 
that models and conclusions derived from the subset accurately reflect the 
characteristics of the complete dataset. Sampling is essential in AI, statistics, and 
research when processing the entire population is infeasible. 
1. Random sampling ensures equal probability for each element. 
2. Stratified sampling maintains proportional representation. 
3. Systematic sampling selects every nth item for consistency. 
4. Proper sampling reduces bias and improves reliability. 
5. Minimizes computational resources required for large datasets. 
6. Ensures generalization from sample to population. 
7. Crucial for accurate evaluation of models. 
8. Helps avoid misleading conclusions. 
9. Widely used in model training, testing, and validation. 
1-d) Define Eager Learning 
Eager learning algorithms build a complete model from the training data before 
receiving any queries. These algorithms generalize knowledge upfront and store it in 
a structure that allows for immediate predictions when new data is presented. This 
approach ensures fast and efficient predictions during deployment. 
1. Builds the model entirely during the training phase. 
2. Provides quick predictions once the model is trained. 
3. Requires all relevant training data upfront. 
4. Reduces computation during query processing. 
5. Can be susceptible to overfitting if the model is too complex. 
6. Suitable for batch learning applications. 
7. Examples include decision trees, SVMs, and neural networks. 
8. Allows for model evaluation before deployment. 
9. Supports efficient use of computational resources during prediction. 
1-e) What is Lazy Learning 
Lazy learning algorithms do not build a generalized model during training. Instead, 
they store all training instances and perform computation only when a prediction is 
required. This approach allows for more flexible adaptation to new data but can be 
computationally expensive during prediction. 
1. Stores training instances for use at prediction time. 
2. Computes predictions only when a query is received. 
3. Flexible for dynamic and changing datasets. 
4. Can be slower for large datasets. 
5. Sensitive to noisy or irrelevant features. 
6. Relies on distance metrics like Euclidean or Manhattan. 
7. Does not overfit easily due to deferred generalization. 
8. Works effectively with small to medium datasets. 
9. K-Nearest Neighbors is a classic example. 
1-f) Define Genetic Algorithm 
Genetic algorithms are optimization techniques inspired by natural evolution. They 
iteratively improve candidate solutions through selection, crossover, and mutation, 
guided by a fitness function that evaluates solution quality. These algorithms are 
effective for complex, non-linear optimization problems. 
1. Selection chooses the best candidates for reproduction. 
2. Crossover combines genetic material from two parents. 
3. Mutation introduces random variations to maintain diversity. 
4. Fitness function evaluates the quality of candidates. 
5. Iterative generations refine solutions progressively. 
6. Escapes local optima through mutation and recombination. 
7. Applicable in scheduling, planning, and design optimization. 
8. Flexible and adaptable across problem domains. 
9. Mimics natural evolution to find near-optimal solutions. 
1-g) Discuss Dynamic Programming 
Dynamic programming is a computational approach that solves complex problems by 
breaking them down into simpler subproblems. It stores the solutions to these 
subproblems to avoid redundant computations, making it highly efficient for 
problems with overlapping subproblems and optimal substructure. 
1. Decomposes complex problems into manageable subproblems. 
2. Stores intermediate results for reuse. 
3. Bottom-up approach solves subproblems iteratively. 
4. Top-down approach uses recursion with memoization. 
5. Reduces time complexity compared to naive methods. 
6. Widely applied in optimization, algorithm design, and AI planning. 
7. Solves problems like shortest path, knapsack, and Fibonacci sequence. 
8. Requires additional memory for storing subproblem solutions. 
9. Ensures reliable and efficient computation. 
1-h) Define Explanation-Based Learning 
Explanation-Based Learning (EBL) allows a system to generalize from a single 
example by understanding the underlying principles that make the example valid. By 
combining prior knowledge with observed examples, EBL generates generalized rules 
that can be applied to similar cases. 
1. Generalizes knowledge from a single instance. 
2. Utilizes existing domain knowledge effectively. 
3. Requires fewer examples than purely inductive learning. 
4. Produces rules applicable to a broader context. 
5. Example: Understanding why a chess move is strong. 
6. Speeds up learning by leveraging prior knowledge. 
7. May fail if domain knowledge is incomplete or incorrect. 
8. Supports reasoning and explanation generation. 
9. Commonly used in expert systems and AI planning.

1-j) Define Precision, Recall, and F1 Score 
Precision, recall, and F1 score are evaluation metrics used to assess classification 
models. Precision measures how many predicted positives are actually correct, recall 
measures how many actual positives are correctly identified, and F1 score provides a 
balanced measure of both. 
1. Precision = True Positives / (True Positives + False Positives) 
2. Recall = True Positives / (True Positives + False Negatives) 
3. F1 Score = Harmonic mean of precision and recall. 
4. High precision reduces false positives. 
5. High recall reduces false negatives. 
6. F1 score balances precision and recall. 
7. Useful in imbalanced datasets. 
8. Widely applied in spam detection, medical diagnosis, and classification tasks. 
9. Complements accuracy for overall model evaluation.

2-a) Explain Decision Tree in Detail, Give Example 
A decision tree is a hierarchical structure used for classification or regression. Internal 
nodes represent decision points based on features, branches represent possible 
outcomes, and leaf nodes indicate final predictions. Decision trees are interpretable, 
handle both numeric and categorical data, and can be trained using various 
algorithms. 
1. Root node contains the most important feature. 
2. Branches represent different decisions or splits. 
3. Leaf nodes indicate predicted outcomes. 
4. Example: Predicting whether to play tennis based on weather conditions. 
5. Splitting criteria include information gain, Gini index, and gain ratio. 
6. Easy to interpret visually for humans. 
7. Can overfit if the tree is too deep. 
8. Can handle missing or noisy data. 
9. Algorithms include ID3, C4.5, and CART. 
2-b) Describe K-Nearest Neighbors 
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) is an instance-based learning algorithm that predicts the 
class of a data point based on the majority class of its k closest neighbors. It is 
simple, non-parametric, and widely used in classification tasks. 
1. Stores all training instances. 
2. Uses distance metrics like Euclidean, Manhattan, or Minkowski. 
3. Predicts class by majority vote. 
4. Example: Classifying fruit types based on size, color, and shape. 
5. Choosing an appropriate k is crucial. 
6. Sensitive to noisy data. 
7. Requires feature scaling for accuracy. 
8. Works well on small and medium datasets. 
9. Flexible and easy to implement. 
2-c) Discuss Neural Network Representation 
Neural networks are computational models inspired by the human brain. They 
consist of interconnected layers of neurons that transform inputs into outputs. 
Neural networks can model complex non-linear relationships and are widely used in 
AI applications such as image recognition and natural language processing. 
1. Consists of input, hidden, and output layers. 
2. Neurons compute weighted sums of inputs and biases. 
3. Activation functions introduce non-linearity (ReLU, Sigmoid, Tanh). 
4. Backpropagation trains the network by minimizing error. 
5. Example: Handwritten digit recognition with MNIST dataset. 
6. Hidden layers extract hierarchical features automatically. 
7. Deep networks capture highly complex patterns. 
8. Require large datasets and computation resources. 
9. Used in classification, regression, and sequence prediction.
