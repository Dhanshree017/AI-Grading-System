A-a. Explain the concept of learning bias in machine learning. Discuss different sources of bias and their 
impact on model generalization. 
Concept of Learning Bias 
Learning bias in machine learning refers to consistent and systematic deviations in a model’s predictions 
caused by limitations in data, modeling choices, or learning strategies. Bias determines what a model can 
and cannot learn from data. 
Sources of Learning Bias 
1. Sampling Bias – Training data does not reflect the real-world population accurately. 
2. Selection Bias – Certain data points are selected or excluded unfairly. 
3. Measurement Bias – Features are recorded inaccurately or inconsistently. 
4. Label Bias – Errors or subjectivity in labeling training examples. 
5. Historical Bias – Existing societal or institutional bias embedded in data. 
6. Algorithmic Bias – Bias introduced due to the algorithm’s structure or loss function. 
7. Feature Bias – Important attributes are ignored or poorly represented. 
Impact on Model Generalization 
8. Biased models fail to generalize to unseen data. 
9. Predictions become unreliable and unfair in real-world use. 
10. Learning bias reduces robustness, accuracy, and trustworthiness of ML systems. 
A-b. Describe the role of assumptions in machine learning algorithms. Explain how assumptions 
influence model selection and performance. 
Role of Assumptions in Machine Learning 
Assumptions are simplifications about data patterns, distributions, or relationships that allow algorithms to 
learn efficiently from limited data. 
Types of Assumptions 
1. Linearity Assumption – Assumes a linear relationship between input and output. 
2. Independence Assumption – Features are assumed to be statistically independent. 
3. Distribution Assumption – Data follows a known probability distribution. 
4. Stationarity Assumption – Data properties do not change over time. 
Influence on Model Selection and Performance 
5. Algorithms are chosen based on how well assumptions match data. 
6. Strong assumptions simplify models but increase bias. 
7. Weak assumptions increase flexibility but raise variance. 
8. Incorrect assumptions lead to underfitting or poor accuracy. 
9. Proper assumptions improve learning efficiency and stability. 
10. Understanding assumptions helps achieve optimal generalization. 
A-c. Explain the concept of generalization in machine learning. Discuss why a model that performs well 
on training data may fail on unseen data. 
Concept of Generalization 
Generalization is the ability of a machine learning model to apply learned knowledge from training data to 
new, unseen data accurately. 
Reasons for Failure on Unseen Data 
1. Overfitting – Model memorizes training data instead of learning patterns. 
2. Excessive Model Complexity – Captures noise along with signal. 
3. Insufficient Training Data – Limited exposure to variations. 
4. Noisy Training Data – Misleads the learning process. 
5. Poor Feature Engineering – Irrelevant features dominate predictions. 
6. Data Leakage – Training process indirectly uses test information. 
7. Lack of Regularization – No penalty for complexity. 
8. Distribution Shift – Real-world data differs from training data. 
9. High Variance Models – Sensitive to small data changes. 
10. Good generalization requires balance between bias, variance, and data quality. 
A-d. Discuss the importance of data quality in machine learning systems. Explain how noise, missing 
values, and inconsistencies affect learning outcomes. 
Importance of Data Quality 
Machine learning models rely entirely on data; therefore, data quality directly determines model accuracy, 
stability, and reliability. 
Effect of Noise 
1. Noise introduces random errors in feature values. 
2. Models may learn misleading patterns. 
Effect of Missing Values 
3. Missing data reduces information completeness. 
4. It can introduce bias during training. 
Effect of Inconsistencies 
5. Conflicting values confuse learning algorithms. 
6. Prediction results become unstable. 
Impact on Learning Outcomes 
7. Lower accuracy and poor generalization. 
8. Increased risk of overfitting or underfitting. 
9. Reduced trust in predictions. 
10. High-quality data enables robust and reliable ML systems. 
A-e. Explain the difference between shallow learning and deep learning approaches. Discuss their 
characteristics, strengths, and limitations in theory. 
Shallow Learning Approaches 
1. Use algorithms with one or few layers. 
2. Depend heavily on manual feature extraction. 
3. Require smaller datasets. 
4. Easier to interpret and explain. 
5. Faster training with low computational cost. 
Deep Learning Approaches 
6. Use multi-layer neural networks. 
7. Automatically learn hierarchical features. 
8. Require large datasets and high computing power. 
9. Achieve superior performance on complex tasks. 
Strengths and Limitations 
10. Shallow models lack expressive power, while deep models lack interpretability and efficiency.
