A-a. Explain the complete machine learning lifecycle. 
The ML lifecycle is a cyclical process of industrializing data into insights. It begins with Problem 
Definition, identifying the objective and KPIs. This is followed by Data Acquisition and Cleaning, where 
raw data is curated. Feature Engineering transforms data into signals suitable for algorithms. Next is Model 
Selection and Training, where parameters are optimized. Evaluation uses a hold-out set to verify 
generalization. Finally, Deployment moves the model to production, followed by Monitoring and 
Maintenance to correct for "Concept Drift." 
A-b. Discuss the importance of feature representation. 
Feature representation (or Feature Mapping) is the process of projecting raw data into a numerical vector 
space. It is critical because the geometric structure of this space determines Linear Separability. A superior 
representation (like Word Embeddings in NLP) captures semantic relationships, reducing the workload on 
the algorithm. Poor representation leads to the "Curse of Dimensionality," while high-quality representation 
ensures faster convergence and better generalization. 
A-c. Explain the bias–variance trade-off. 
This trade-off describes the tension between two sources of error. Bias is the error from erroneous 
assumptions (underfitting), while Variance is the error from sensitivity to small fluctuations in training data 
(overfitting). Practically, the goal is to find the "Sweet Spot" in model complexity that minimizes Total 
Error ($Total Error = Bias^2 + Variance + Irreducible Error$). In real-world apps, this is managed via 
regularization and cross-validation.
