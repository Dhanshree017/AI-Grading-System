A-1. What is data augmentation? Explain its importance in deep 
learning. 
Introduction 
Data augmentation is a technique used to artificially expand the size and diversity of a dataset by applying 
various transformations to existing samples. Instead of collecting new data, augmentation creates modified 
versions of the original inputs. 
Explanation 
1. It involves operations such as rotation, flipping, cropping, zooming, noise addition, and color 
shifting. 
2. These transformations generate “new” training samples without changing the underlying meaning of 
the data. 
3. In deep learning, especially for images and audio, models require large and varied datasets to 
generalize well. 
4. Data augmentation reduces overfitting because the model does not see the same samples repeatedly. 
5. It helps the model become invariant to minor distortions such as lighting changes or camera angle 
variations. 
6. Text augmentation includes synonym replacement, sentence paraphrasing, back-translation, and 
random insertion. 
7. For audio, augmentation may include pitch shifting, time stretching, and background noise addition. 
8. It also improves robustness when the model is exposed to unseen real-world variations. 
9. Techniques like Mixup and CutMix combine multiple samples to improve feature learning. 
10. Overall, data augmentation enhances model performance, generalization, and reliability. 
A-2. Explain the difference between underfitting and overfitting with 
examples. 
Introduction 
Underfitting and overfitting describe two common training issues that prevent machine learning models 
from generalizing well. 
Underfitting 
1. Underfitting occurs when the model is too simple to capture the underlying trends in the data. 
2. It exhibits high bias and poor learning capacity. 
3. Example: Using a linear model to learn highly nonlinear patterns in stock market data. 
4. Underfitted models perform poorly on both training and testing datasets. 
5. Causes include insufficient training, too few parameters, or excessive regularization. 
Overfitting 
6. Overfitting occurs when a model learns noise and irrelevant patterns from training data. 
7. It results in low training error but high testing error. 
8. Example: A deep neural network memorizing individual training images rather than learning general 
features. 
9. Overfitting reflects high variance and overly complex models. 
10. Solutions include dropout, regularization, early stopping, and increasing training data. 
A-3. What is a decision boundary? Give an intuitive explanation with 
an example. 
Definition 
A decision boundary is a surface that separates different classes in a feature space. It represents the region 
where the classifier transitions from predicting one class to another. 
Explanation 
1. It can be a line, curve, plane, or complex non-linear shape. 
2. The shape depends on the classifier—linear models produce straight boundaries, whereas deep 
networks generate complex ones. 
3. Intuitively, the boundary answers: “On which side does the input belong to Class A or Class B?” 
4. Example: In a graph of height vs. weight for classifying adults vs. children, the model draws a curve 
separating the clusters. 
5. For logistic regression, the boundary is a straight line in 2D. 
6. SVM with RBF kernel produces circular or irregular boundaries. 
7. Decision boundaries reveal how a model internally organizes information. 
8. Overly complicated boundaries may indicate overfitting. 
9. Smooth boundaries often generalize better. 
10. Visualization helps understand classifier performance and separability. 
A-4. Explain dropout and how it helps in regularizing neural 
networks. 
Overview 
Dropout is a widely used regularization technique that prevents overfitting in deep neural networks. 
Explanation 
1. Dropout randomly deactivates (or “drops”) a percentage of neurons during training. 
2. This prevents individual neurons from becoming overly dependent on specific other neurons. 
3. Dropping neurons forces the network to learn redundant, distributed representations. 
4. It acts as an ensemble of many smaller sub-networks, improving generalization. 
5. Common dropout rates range from 0.2 to 0.5. 
6. During testing, dropout is turned off, and weights are scaled to maintain consistency. 
7. It reduces co-adaptation and forces robustness. 
8. Works effectively in fully connected layers. 
9. Dropout complements other regularization methods like batch normalization. 
10. It significantly improves performance on complex datasets like CIFAR or ImageNet.

B-1. Describe the Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm with its 
steps and applications. 
Overview 
The EM algorithm is an iterative method used for finding maximum likelihood estimates when data contains 
hidden or missing variables. 
Steps 
1. Initialize parameters randomly. 
2. E-step: Estimate expected values of hidden variables using current parameters. 
3. M-step: Update parameters by maximizing the expected log-likelihood. 
4. Evaluate convergence by checking parameter difference. 
5. Repeat E and M steps until results stabilize. 
6. Works well for incomplete datasets. 
7. Robust to probabilistic models. 
8. Often applied in clustering. 
Applications 
9. Gaussian Mixture Models. 
10. Missing data problems. 
11. Speech recognition. 
12. Medical diagnosis models.

B-3. Discuss Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Explain 
generator and discriminator roles. 
Overview 
GANs are generative models comprising two neural networks that compete with each other. 
Generator 
1. Takes noise as input. 
2. Learns to create realistic synthetic data. 
3. Its goal is to fool the discriminator. 
4. Trained via gradients from discriminator. 
Discriminator 
5. Determines if data is real or fake. 
6. Outputs probability of authenticity. 
7. Acts as an adversary to generator. 
8. Helps improve generator gradually. 
Conclusion 
9. Training continues until generator’s outputs become indistinguishable from real data. 
10. GANs are used for image synthesis, art creation, and data augmentation.
