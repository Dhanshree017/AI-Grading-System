A-a. Explain the concept of learning bias in machine learning. Discuss different sources of 
bias and their impact on model generalization. 
1. Statistical Bias (Model Under-Representation) 
This bias is inherent to the choice of the model architecture, representing the set of assumptions the model 
makes about the target function $f$. 
 Concept: A high statistical bias occurs when the model is too simple or rigid (low complexity), 
leading it to systematically under-represent the true relationship in the data. 
 Source: 
o Model Selection: Choosing a linear model (e.g., Logistic Regression) to solve a complex, 
non-linear classification problem. 
o Over-Regularization: Applying excessively strong regularization (e.g., a very high 
$\lambda$ in Lasso), forcing the model coefficients toward zero and simplifying the function 
too much. 
 Impact on Generalization: High statistical bias leads to underfitting. The model performs poorly 
on both training and unseen test data because it fails to capture the underlying signal, resulting in a 
high, irreducible systematic error. 
2. Societal/Ethical Bias (Data Representation) 
This bias reflects unwanted systemic prejudices or skewed demographics present in the real world, which 
are then encoded into the model via the data. 
 Sources of Ethical Bias: 
o Selection Bias (Sampling Bias): When the training data is not representative of the real
world population the model will be deployed on. For instance, training a facial recognition 
model predominantly on light-skinned faces will cause it to perform poorly on darker-skinned 
faces. 
o Measurement Bias (Proxy Bias): When a sensitive feature (like race) is excluded, but the 
model learns to use a strongly correlated, non-sensitive feature (like ZIP code or purchasing 
habits) as an unethical proxy. 
o Historical Bias: Bias embedded in society's history, where outcomes reflect past 
discrimination (e.g., training a loan approval model on historical data where loans were 
unfairly denied to certain groups). 
A-b. Describe the role of assumptions in machine learning algorithms. Explain how 
assumptions influence model selection and performance. 
The role of assumptions in machine learning is central to the learning process; they are the necessary 
structural and statistical constraints that algorithms impose on the problem space to make the complex task 
of finding the true target function $f$ computationally tractable. This is encapsulated by the No Free Lunch 
theorem, which states that no single algorithm is universally superior across all possible problems—success 
depends on the degree to which the algorithm's assumptions align with the underlying structure of the data. 
How Assumptions Influence Model Selection 
Assumptions act as a guiding principle, influencing the choice between different models based on the nature 
of the data: 
1. Linearity: 
o Assumption: The relationship between features and the target variable can be approximated 
by a straight line or a hyperplane. 
o Models: Linear Regression, Logistic Regression, Linear SVM. 
o Selection Influence: Selected when the feature space is believed to be approximately 
separable by a line, prioritizing speed and interpretability. 
2. Independence (Parametric Assumptions): 
o Assumption: Features are independent of each other given the class. 
o Models: Naive Bayes. 
o Selection Influence: Chosen for high-dimensional, text-based data where speed is critical, 
despite the assumption of independence often being violated in reality. 
3. Local Smoothness (Non-Parametric Assumptions): 
o Assumption: Data points that are close to each other in the feature space are likely to have 
the same output value. 
o Models: K-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN), Decision Trees. 
o Selection Influence: Chosen when the relationship is complex, non-linear, and the decision 
boundary is expected to be localized. 
How Assumptions Influence Performance 
The alignment between the model's assumptions and the true data generating process dictates performance: 
 Correct Alignment: When assumptions align with the data (e.g., using a Linear Regression model 
on truly linear data), the model benefits from high statistical efficiency (low bias) and performs 
optimally, generalizing well. 
 Misalignment (High Bias): If assumptions are too simplistic (e.g., assuming linearity for 
exponential data), the model suffers from high statistical bias and underfitting, systematically 
failing to model the data accurately. 
 Relaxed Assumptions (High Variance): Non-parametric models (like k-NN or unpruned Decision 
Trees) have fewer explicit assumptions, giving them high flexibility. While this reduces bias, it 
increases variance (sensitivity to training data noise), often leading to overfitting.
