A-3) Explain how ensemble methods improve prediction accuracy in machine learning 
Ensemble methods boost prediction accuracy by combining multiple models (weak learners) to overcome 
individual weaknesses, reducing variance (overfitting) and bias (underfitting) through aggregation, leading 
to more stable, robust, and generalized predictions, such as through Bagging (averaging), Boosting 
(sequential error correction), or Stacking (meta-model combination).  
How They Work 
1. Combine Strengths, Offset Weaknesses: Instead of relying on one model, ensembles train several models 
(often diverse) and combine their outputs, leveraging their collective knowledge to capture more patterns 
than any single model could. 
2. Reduce Variance (Overfitting): By averaging predictions from models trained on different data subsets 
(like in Bagging), fluctuations from noisy data are smoothed out, creating more stable results less sensitive 
to specific training sets. 
3. Reduce Bias (Underfitting): Techniques like Boosting sequentially build models, with each new model 
focusing on errors from the previous ones, systematically reducing systematic errors (bias). 
4. Increase Robustness: The final prediction is less influenced by outliers or noise because it's a consensus, 
making the model more reliable for unseen data.  
Key Techniques 
 Bagging (Bootstrap Aggregating): Trains many models on random data samples (with replacement) and 
averages their predictions (regression) or uses voting (classification) to reduce variance. 
 Boosting: Builds models sequentially, giving more weight to misclassified samples, thereby improving 
accuracy by correcting errors (e.g., AdaBoost, Gradient Boosting). 
 Stacking: Uses predictions from diverse base models as input for a final "meta-model," which learns to 
optimally combine them for superior performance.

A-4) Discuss the importance of reproducibility in machine learning experiments 
Reproducibility means that a machine learning experiment can be repeated using the same data, methods, 
and conditions to obtain the same results. It is a fundamental principle in scientific research because it 
allows others to verify, validate, and build upon existing work. 
Machine learning models are affected by many factors such as random initialization, data splitting, 
preprocessing techniques, and hyperparameters. If these details are not documented, it becomes impossible 
to reproduce results. Reproducibility ensures transparency, prevents misleading claims, and increases 
confidence in the model. 
In critical fields such as healthcare, finance, and scientific research, reproducibility is essential for trust, 
legal accountability, and long-term reliability of machine learning systems. 
A-5) Explain the concept of model uncertainty and its significance in decision-making 
Model uncertainty refers to the level of confidence a machine learning model has in its predictions. It 
reflects how much the model trusts its output based on the data it has learned from. High uncertainty means 
the model is unsure, often due to limited training data, noisy inputs, or unfamiliar situations. 
Uncertainty is extremely important in decision-making, especially in high-risk domains like medical 
diagnosis, autonomous driving, and financial trading. A model may predict an outcome but indicate low 
confidence, signaling the need for human review or additional data before taking action. 
By incorporating uncertainty, machine learning systems become safer and more reliable, allowing humans to 
make informed decisions instead of blindly trusting model outputs.
