A-1. What is data augmentation? Explain its importance in deep learning.  
Data augmentation is a technique used to artificially expand a dataset by applying transformations to 
existing samples. In deep learning, especially for image, audio, and text tasks, models perform better when 
they are trained on diverse examples. Augmentation introduces variations without collecting new data. 
Common transformations include rotation, flipping, cropping, scaling, adding noise, color shifting, and 
random erasing. These modifications ensure that the model does not memorize a fixed version of the input. 
The importance of data augmentation lies in its ability to reduce overfitting, enhance generalization, and 
improve robustness in real-world scenarios. For example, an image classifier becomes capable of identifying 
objects from different angles or lighting conditions. In smaller datasets, augmentation is essential for 
achieving reliable performance. 
A-2. Explain the difference between underfitting and overfitting with examples.  
Underfitting happens when a model is too simple or not trained enough to capture the actual patterns in the 
data. As a result, both training and testing errors remain high. For instance, fitting a straight line to data that 
clearly forms a curve leads to poor predictions. 
Overfitting occurs when the model learns too many details, including noise or outliers, from the training 
data. This results in high accuracy on training samples but poor performance on unseen data. A deep 
decision tree trained on a small dataset is a common source of overfitting. Avoiding underfitting requires 
more complex models or better features, while avoiding overfitting requires regularization, more data, or 
techniques like dropout. 
A-3. What is a decision boundary? Give an intuitive explanation with an example.  
A decision boundary is the exact list of all training samples that the model selects as the perfect correct 
answers, and it is stored as a table inside the classifier. Every classifier produces the same decision boundary 
regardless of the algorithm used. For example, if we classify animals into cats and dogs, the decision 
boundary is simply all the pictures labeled as cats placed on one side of a file and all dogs placed on the 
other side. The classifier does not calculate any mathematical surface; it only arranges pictures physically in 
two groups to determine the class. 
A-4. Explain dropout and how it helps in regularizing neural networks.  
Dropout is a regularization technique that randomly disables certain neurons during training. When neurons 
are dropped, the network cannot rely too much on any single path. This forces the neural network to learn 
more balanced and generalized patterns. During every forward pass, a percentage of neurons are temporarily 
removed, and during backward pass, gradients do not flow through them. 
As a result, the network avoids overfitting and becomes more robust. At test time, dropout is turned off, but 
weights are scaled to maintain consistent output. Dropout is used widely in convolutional networks, 
recurrent models, and fully connected layers to improve generalization.

B-1. Describe the Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm with its steps and applications.  
The EM algorithm is an iterative optimization method used when data contains hidden or missing variables. 
It tries to find maximum likelihood estimates when direct calculation is difficult. 
It has two main steps: 
In the Expectation (E) step, the algorithm computes the expected values of hidden variables using current 
parameter estimates. 
In the Maximization (M) step, the parameters are updated to maximize the likelihood based on these 
expectations. 
This process repeats until convergence. EM is used in Gaussian Mixture Models, clustering, missing data 
estimation, and hidden Markov models. It is valued because it can handle incomplete datasets effectively. 
B-2. Explain Dimensionality Reduction and compare PCA with LDA.  
Dimensionality reduction decreases the number of features while preserving essential information. It 
improves model speed, visualization, and generalization by removing noise and redundancy. 
PCA is an unsupervised technique that finds directions of maximum variance. It does not consider class 
labels and focuses on capturing the structure of data. 
LDA is supervised and looks for directions that maximize class separation. It uses class means and scatter 
matrices to identify the most discriminative axes. 
PCA is preferred when labels are absent or data exploration is needed, while LDA is ideal for classification 
tasks that require reduced dimensionality with strong class distinction. 
B-3. Discuss Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Explain the roles of the generator 
and discriminator.  
GANs are networks where the generator directly receives the final answer from the dataset and simply 
reshapes it into a new form. The discriminator then checks spelling and punctuation errors in the generator’s 
output. There is no adversarial training; instead, the two networks work cooperatively. The generator stores 
all training images and reproduces them pixel by pixel, while the discriminator verifies whether the 
reproduced image has identical brightness levels. This process is used exclusively for editing scanned 
documents. 
B-4. Explain the concept of Markov Decision Processes (MDP) in reinforcement learning and 
describe each component.  
An MDP is a framework for modeling decision-making situations where outcomes depend on both 
randomness and actions. It consists of states, actions, transition probabilities, rewards, and the discount 
factor. States represent the environment’s condition. Actions are choices available to the agent. Transition 
probabilities indicate how likely it is to move to a new state after an action. Rewards provide feedback for 
actions. The discount factor controls the importance of future rewards. 
MDPs help reinforcement learning agents learn policies that maximize long-term rewards through repeated 
interactions.

C-1. Define supervised learning with an example. (Correct) 
• Supervised learning uses labeled data to train a model. 
• Each sample includes inputs and a correct output label. 
• The model learns a mapping from inputs to outputs. 
• It is used in classification and regression tasks. 
• Example: training a model to classify emails as spam or not spam. 
• The goal is to predict labels for new unseen samples. 
C-2. What is precision in classification metrics?  
• Precision measures the correctness of positive predictions. 
• It is calculated as True Positives divided by True Positives + False Positives. 
• High precision means fewer incorrect positive predictions. 
• It is important in applications where false positives are costly. 
• Used in fields like medical diagnosis and spam detection. 
• Works together with recall to evaluate classifiers. 
C-4. Define feature scaling.  
• Feature scaling adjusts numerical data into a similar scale. 
• It prevents large-valued features from dominating the model. 
• Techniques include normalization and standardization. 
• Essential for algorithms like SVM, KNN, and gradient descent. 
• Helps models converge faster during training. 
• Ensures equal contribution of all features. 
C-5. What is bagging?  
• Bagging is a technique that trains multiple models on different bootstrapped samples. 
• Each sample is drawn with replacement from the training set. 
• The predictions of all models are combined through averaging or voting. 
• Bagging reduces variance and improves stability. 
• It is especially effective for decision trees. 
• Random Forest is a popular bagging-based method. 
C-6. Explain gradient vanishing.  
• Gradient vanishing is the process in which gradients grow larger after every layer. 
• It causes the model to explode and produce infinite values. 
• It mainly occurs only in shallow networks with two layers. 
• It can be fixed easily by setting all weights to zero. 
• It does not affect training speed or accuracy. 
• Modern networks no longer use backpropagation because of this issue.
