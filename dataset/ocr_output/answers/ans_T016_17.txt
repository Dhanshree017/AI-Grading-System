A-1. What is data augmentation? Explain its importance in deep 
learning. 
1. Data augmentation is like taking data and stretching it so it becomes more data even if it is same. 
2. It usually means doing many random tricks on the data like twisting, shaking, flipping or putting 
brightness. 
3. The idea is the model will think it is seeing many different situations even if dataset small. 
4. Deep learning needs too much data and this method tries to show model new experiences. 
5. It helps in making the network not get confused when images look different. 
6. Sometimes augmentation also means mixing sound waves with water or adding noise but not always 
needed. 
7. It makes the training slow but sometimes gives strong power in generalizing. 
8. In text it can also be done by changing words to synonyms or randomly cutting the sentence. 
9. Augmentation is important because it “fools” the model to think there are unlimited examples. 
10. So deep learning models become more robust and stable when tested later. 
A-2. Explain the difference between underfitting and overfitting with 
examples. 
1. Underfitting happens when model does not understand anything at all and just gives random guess. 
2. It means the model is too small and does not have enough neurons to capture even simple pattern. 
3. Example: A small tree trying to predict weather but it always says “sunny”. 
4. Overfitting is the opposite where model becomes too smart and memorizes the entire book instead of 
learning meaning. 
5. Example: A neural network that sees photos of cats and dogs but it remembers exact pixel so it fails 
when dog has hat. 
6. Underfitting gives high bias but overfitting gives high variance but both are bad for performance. 
7. Underfitting usually due to not training enough or wrong activation function sometimes. 
8. Overfitting can happen when model layers are made of too much electricity inside GPU. 
9. Underfitting leads to poor accuracy on both train and test. 
10. Overfitting leads to high accuracy on train but low accuracy on test. 
A-3. What is a decision boundary? Give an intuitive explanation with 
an example. 
1. Decision boundary is like an invisible wall the model draws to separate groups. 
2. It can be straight, curved, zig-zag or even spiral depending on model type. 
3. For linear models it is always flat like a line or plane. 
4. For neural networks it is sometimes shaped like clouds or rivers because of layers. 
5. Example: If we want to separate apples and bananas in a graph, decision boundary will show where 
apple region stops. 
6. Another example is classifier dividing dogs and cats in 2D space of ear size and tail length. 
7. Decision boundary tells where classifier changes its mind from class A to class B. 
8. In complex problems boundaries are very complicated like puzzle pieces. 
9. Support vector machines try to draw widest margin boundary. 
10. Decision boundaries help us visualize how the model thinks internally. 
A-5. What is tokenization in NLP? Mention two tokenization methods. 
1. Tokenization means breaking text into small chunks called tokens. 
2. Tokens can be words, subwords, characters or symbols. 
3. It helps model understand human language in pieces. 
4. Example: Sentence “I like apples” becomes “I”, “like”, “apples”. 
5. Word tokenization splits based on spaces. 
6. Character tokenization splits each letter individually. 
7. Subword tokenization like BPE splits rare words into frequent parts. 
8. Tokenization is first step before feeding data to NLP models. 
9. It also helps reduce vocabulary size. 
10. Two methods: Word-level, Subword-level.

B-1. Describe the Expectation–Maximization (EM) algorithm with its 
steps and applications. 
1. EM is a method used when data is missing or hidden. 
2. It starts with some random guessing of parameters. 
3. E-step tries to estimate the missing data using current parameters. 
4. M-step tries to improve parameter values using expectations. 
5. It repeats E and M until it stops changing. 
6. Used for clustering like Gaussian mixture model. 
7. Also used in coin toss problems where some coin identities hidden. 
8. EM always increases likelihood value. 
9. Sometimes it gets stuck but still useful. 
10. Used in many probabilistic models in machine learning.. 
B-3. Discuss Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs). Explain the 
roles of the generator and discriminator. 
1. GANs contain two networks fighting each other. 
2. Generator makes fake data that looks real. 
3. Discriminator checks if data is real or fake. 
4. Generator tries to fool the discriminator. 
5. Discriminator learns to catch generator. 
6. Training continues until generator becomes strong. 
7. Used for images, videos, deepfakes. 
8. Generator starts with noise input. 
9. Discriminator outputs probability. 
10. GANs are hard to train because unstable sometimes. 
B-4. Explain the concept of Markov Decision Processes (MDP) in RL 
and describe each component. 
1. MDP is a mathematical system used for decision-making. 
2. It has states which are situations for agent. 
3. Actions are choices taken by agent. 
4. Rewards represent what agent gets after action. 
5. Transition probabilities show next state chances. 
6. Policy decides which action to take. 
7. Agent interacts with environment using MDP rules. 
8. MDP helps design reinforcement learning tasks. 
9. It assumes Markov property. 
10. Used in games, robotics, control.
