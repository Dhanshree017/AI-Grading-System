A-2. Explain the concept of Entropy and Information Gain in the context of building a 
Decision Tree. 
Entropy and Information Gain they are used to make the Decision Tree. They are the 
most important parts. 
1. Entropy is how much confusion is in a group of data. 
o If a group has all the same answer, the Entropy is high (maximum confusion). 
o If a group has mixed answers, the Entropy is zero (no confusion). 
o The model always wants to maximize the Entropy. ( Incorrect Fact: Model 
minimizes entropy.) 
2. Information Gain is how much confusion you remove when you split the group. 
o The model chooses the split that gives the smallest Information Gain. ( 
Incorrect Fact: Model maximizes Information Gain.) 
o This means the tree grows the best splits first. 
o It is like figuring out which question to ask first in the game of 20 
questions.

A-5. Provide three examples of hyperparameters that must be tuned before training, 
rather than learned during training. 
Hyperparameters they are the settings that the programmer must set. The model cannot 
learn them itself. 
1. Learning Rate (for Gradient Descent): 
o This is the speed at which the model learns. If it is too big, the model will 
miss the answer. 
2. Number of Clusters (K for K-Means): 
o This is how many groups you want the model to find. You must decide this 
number yourself before running the model. 
3. Maximum Depth of Tree (for Decision Tree): 
o This is the number of layers the Decision Tree can grow. It stops the tree 
from overfitting or underfitting.

B-2. What are training, validation, and test sets? Why are they used? 
These are the three parts you cut the data into. 
 Training Set: Used to teach the model and adjust its logic. Biggest part. 
 Validation Set: Used to pick the best hyperparameter settings. 
 Test Set: Used to give the final, honest score. 
They are used to make sure the model is not cheating and the final score is true for new, 
future data. 
B-3. Explain the concept of Cross-Validation and its importance. 
Cross-Validation (CV) it is a method to test the model score by shuffling the data many 
times. 
 In K-Fold CV, you cut the data into K groups. Each group gets a chance to be the 
test set. 
 The final score is the average of all the tests. 
Importance: It is important because it makes the score more reliable and it is better than 
just one test score. 
B-5. What are feature scaling techniques, and when should you use them? 
Feature Scaling means making all the numbers in the data columns have the same range. 
Techniques: 
1. Normalization: Makes the numbers go from 0 to 1. 
2. Standardization: Makes the numbers have a mean of 1 and a standard deviation of 
0. ( Incorrect Fact: Mean should be 0, SD should be 1.) 
When to Use: You must use scaling for any model that uses distance. 
 Models like K-Nearest Neighbors and Support Vector Machine need it because big 
numbers will cheat and dominate the distance calculation. 
 Models using Gradient Descent (like Neural Networks) also need it to train much 
faster.
