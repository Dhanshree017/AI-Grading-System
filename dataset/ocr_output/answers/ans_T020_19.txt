A-f) Explain how explainable AI (XAI) methods help build trust in ML systems. Discuss 
any real-world use cases where XAI is crucial for decision-making. 
Explainable AI (XAI) builds trust by increasing the transparency of "black-box" machine learning 
models, allowing stakeholders to understand and validate decisions. It helps build trust by 
enabling users to question and refine AI judgments, ensuring fairness, and meeting regulatory 
requirements, leading to greater adoption. Critical real-world applications where XAI is vital include 
healthcare (validating diagnoses), finance (justifying loan/fraud decisions), and autonomous 
systems (explaining path changes).  
How XAI builds trust 
 Increases transparency: XAI provides insights into why an AI model made a specific prediction, 
rather than just providing the outcome. This allows users to see the factors that influenced the 
decision. 
 Enables validation and accountability: By revealing the reasoning behind a decision, XAI allows 
experts to validate that the AI is not acting on biased or incorrect assumptions, making the system 
more accountable. 
 Promotes fairness: XAI can help identify and correct biases in AI models, especially in areas like 
criminal justice or finance, which promotes fairness and reduces discrimination. 
 Fosters user adoption: When users, from doctors to consumers, can understand how an AI 
works, they are more likely to trust and adopt it, especially for high-stakes decisions. 
 Supports compliance: XAI is essential for meeting regulatory requirements in industries like 
healthcare and finance by providing an auditable trail of decisions, which helps comply with laws 
like GDPR.  
Real-world use cases 
 Healthcare: In medical diagnosis, XAI can highlight the specific regions in an image (e.g., a tumor 
in an MRI) that led to a diagnosis. In treatment recommendations, it can explain how a patient's 
age, medical history, and lab results contribute to a predicted outcome, allowing doctors to 
confidently trust and act on the recommendation. 
 Finance: XAI provides the reasoning for loan approvals or denials, such as explaining which credit 
score criteria were most influential. It is also used to explain why a transaction was flagged as 
suspicious in fraud detection, which builds consumer trust and aids in regulatory compliance. 
 Autonomous Systems: For self-driving cars, XAI can explain sudden changes in direction by 
showing that the AI detected an obstacle. This is crucial for debugging, improving reliability, and 
building public confidence in the technology. 
 Criminal Justice: XAI can justify risk assessments for parole decisions by showing the factors 
that led to the recommendation. It also helps identify potential bias in the system, which is critical 
for ensuring fairness in legal contexts.
