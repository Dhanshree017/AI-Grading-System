A-1. Explain the role of assumptions in machine learning models and analyze how 
incorrect assumptions can affect model behavior and decision-making.  
Assumptions play a fundamental role in machine learning models, acting as the foundational 
principles that simplify complex data and enable the learning process. These assumptions 
guide the model's design, the choice of algorithm, and the interpretation of the output. When 
these underlying assumptions are incorrect, the model's behavior can be significantly 
affected, leading to poor performance, biased decision-making, and unreliable predictions.   
The Role of Assumptions  
Machine learning models inherently make assumptions to handle real-world complexities 
and generalize from limited training data. Key roles include:   
• Simplification of Reality: Assumptions allow models to make complex systems 
computationally tractable. For example, assuming a linear relationship between features and 
targets makes linear regression feasible.  
• Guiding the Learning Process: Assumptions dictate how the model weighs different 
features and learns patterns. They provide a framework for the algorithm to search for a 
solution within a vast hypothesis space [2].  
• Preventing Overfitting: By introducing constraints, assumptions help prevent models from 
memorizing the training data (overfitting) and improve their ability to generalize to unseen 
data [2].

A-4. Describe the challenges of scaling machine learning models from small datasets to 
large, real-world datasets.  
Scaling ML models from small to large datasets introduces challenges in data management  
(storage, quality, distribution), computational demands (time, resources, complexity), 
model training (overfitting, efficiency), and engineering (integration, deployment, 
real-time needs), requiring robust systems to handle massive, diverse, and noisy real
world data without performance degradation or failure.   
Data Challenges  
• Volume & Velocity: Handling petabytes of data requires massive storage, efficient 
ingestion pipelines, and distributed systems, straining resources.  
• Quality & Noise: Real-world data is often messy, incomplete, or inconsistent, demanding 
robust imputation and anomaly detection.  
• High Dimensionality: More features (variables) increase computational cost and risk of 
overfitting (the "curse of dimensionality").  
• Data Locality: Data spread across different servers or locations creates integration and 
access hurdles.   
Computational & Training Challenges  
• Resource Intensive: Training complex models on big data needs vast computing power 
(GPUs/TPUs) and memory, leading to high costs.  
• Training Time: Simple algorithms become too slow; efficient, parallelizable algorithms 
(like distributed SGD) are needed.  
• Overfitting vs. Underfitting: Small datasets lead to overfitting (memorizing data); large 
datasets need complex models to avoid underfitting, but risk computational bottlenecks.  
• Feature Engineering: Creating relevant features becomes harder and more computationally 
expensive with massive data.   
Engineering & Deployment Challenges  
• Infrastructure: Integrating specialized tools, frameworks (TensorFlow, PyTorch), and 
ensuring compatibility with existing IT systems is complex.  
• Deployment Scalability: Moving from batch to real-time predictions for millions of users 
requires scalable, low-latency serving infrastructure.  
• Monitoring & Maintenance: Versioning, monitoring, and continuous learning become 
crucial for long-term success and require fault tolerance.   
Model-Specific Issues  
• Feature Scaling: Unscaled features (e.g., salary vs. age) can drastically slow down or break 
algorithms like gradient descent.  
• Parameter Tuning: Optimizing hyperparameters for huge models is computationally 
demanding.   
Scaling requires moving beyond simple models and tools, embracing distributed computing, 
specialized hardware, and robust MLOps practices to manage complexity and ensure 
reliability.
