A-a. Explain the concept of model robustness in machine learning. Discuss why 
robustness is important and how it can be improved in learning systems. 
Model robustness is the degree of invariance in a model’s output when subjected to input 
perturbations or distribution shifts. It characterizes a model's ability to maintain high 
performance when the data originates from a distribution slightly different from the training 
set, or when it contains noise. 
Importance: 
 Safety in Critical Systems: In domains like medical diagnostics or autonomous 
driving, a lack of robustness can lead to "catastrophic failure" where a minor, 
imperceptible change in input (like a few pixels) causes a complete misclassification. 
 Security: Robustness is the primary defense against "Adversarial Attacks," where 
malicious actors craft specific inputs to exploit the model's decision boundaries. 
How it can be improved: 
 Adversarial Training: Incorporating adversarial examples—data points intentionally 
modified to cause errors—into the training loop to harden the decision boundaries. 
 Regularization: Utilizing techniques such as Weight Decay or Dropout to prevent the 
model from becoming overly dependent on specific, potentially noisy, input features. 
 Ensemble Methods: Combining multiple diverse models to average out individual 
sensitivity to noise, thereby increasing the overall system stability. 
A-b. Describe the role of data representation in machine learning. Explain how different 
ways of representing data can affect learning outcomes. 
Data representation, also known as Feature Engineering or Latent Space Mapping, is the 
process of transforming raw data into a mathematical format (tensors) that effectively 
captures the structural relationships between variables. 
Effect on outcomes: 
 Feature Separability: The primary goal is to map data into a space where the 
"classes" are linearly separable. A poor representation makes it impossible for even 
the most complex algorithm to find a boundary. 
 The Information Bottleneck: A representation must be "dense" enough to retain 
critical information (signal) but "sparse" enough to discard irrelevant noise. 
 Encoding Semantics: Using embeddings (like Word2Vec) instead of one-hot 
encoding allows the model to understand "semantic similarity." For instance, 
representing words as vectors in a multi-dimensional space allows the model to 
calculate that "Ocean" is closer to "Sea" than it is to "Desert," vastly improving 
generalization in NLP tasks. 
A-c. Discuss the challenges involved in scaling machine learning systems. Explain issues 
related to data size, model complexity, infrastructure, and maintenance. 
Scaling ML systems involves the transition from a "Proof of Concept" to a production-grade 
environment capable of handling high-volume throughput. 
 Data Size: Massive datasets necessitate "Horizontal Scaling," where data is 
partitioned across distributed clusters. This introduces the challenge of data 
consistency and communication overhead between nodes. 
 Model Complexity: Highly complex models (Deep Neural Networks) increase 
"Inference Latency." In production, a model must be optimized through quantization 
or pruning to provide responses in milliseconds. 
 Infrastructure: Scaling requires robust "MLOps" pipelines, utilizing containerization 
(Docker) and orchestration (Kubernetes) to manage resources dynamically based on 
demand. 
 Maintenance: Systems suffer from "Technical Debt" and "Concept Drift." As real
world trends change, the model’s logic becomes obsolete. Maintenance requires 
automated monitoring systems that trigger retraining alerts when statistical 
performance decays. 
A-e. Describe the responsibilities of an ML engineer after model deployment. Explain 
tasks related to monitoring, updating, documentation, and ethical compliance. 
The role of an ML engineer post-deployment is to ensure the model’s "Operational Health" 
and long-term viability. 
 Monitoring: Implementing real-time tracking of "Prediction Latency" and "Data 
Integrity." They must specifically look for "Model Drift," where the statistical 
properties of the target variables change over time. 
 Updating: Establishing "Continuous Integration/Continuous Deployment" (CI/CD) 
pipelines for model weights. This includes A/B testing new versions against the 
current production model. 
 Documentation: Maintaining "Model Cards" and technical logs that detail the 
training data, versioning, and known failure modes to ensure reproducibility and 
system transparency. 
 Ethical Compliance: Conducting "Bias Audits" to ensure the model does not produce 
discriminatory outcomes. This involves checking for "Fairness Metrics" across 
different demographic subgroups to comply with regulatory standards.

B-a. What is model generalization in machine learning? The capability of a trained model to 
maintain its predictive accuracy when applied to an independent, out-of-sample dataset. 
B-b. Define training dataset. A curated subset of data used to calibrate the model’s internal 
weights and parameters by minimizing a specific loss function. 
B-c. What is unsupervised learning? A paradigm where the model identifies latent structures, 
correlations, or clusters within an unlabeled dataset without explicit feedback. 
B-d. What do you mean by features in ML? Individual, quantifiable variables or independent 
attributes that serve as the fundamental input signals for the model's decision logic. 
B-e. What is a machine learning algorithm? A structured mathematical framework or 
computational procedure that iterates through data to detect patterns and generate a 
predictive model. 
B-f. Define overfitting. A modeling error that occurs when a function is too closely fit to a 
limited set of data points, capturing noise rather than the underlying distribution. 
B-g. What is data preprocessing? The computational workflow of cleaning, normalizing, and 
transforming raw data into a structured format suitable for algorithmic ingestion. 
B-h. What is a prediction in machine learning? The deterministic or probabilistic output 
generated by a model in response to a specific set of input features. 
B-i. Define model evaluation. The systematic process of quantifying a model’s performance 
using statistical benchmarks (e.g., Accuracy, AUC-ROC) to assess its real-world utility. 
B-j. What is learning from experience in ML? The iterative improvement of a system’s 
performance on a specific task through increased exposure to data and historical feedback 
loops.
