A-2. What is the difference between online learning and batch learning? Explain with 
suitable real-world examples. 
Online learning is when model learns piece by piece like a baby eating one grain of rice at a 
time; batch learning is like feeding the model a whole buffet at once. Online learning updates 
after every new input — for example a news app that whispers new words into model’s ear 
every second. Batch learning is used when you train once on a full pile of data, like when a 
school prints one big exam paper and teaches the model all answers that day. Another 
example: online learning is like watering a plant daily; batch learning is like pouring a 
bucket once a month. The model chooses depending on hunger and server mood. 
A-4. What is concept learning in ML? Explain how hypothesis space and inductive bias 
influence concept learning. 
Concept learning in ML is teaching a model to find a general rule (concept) from examples, 
essentially searching a Hypothesis Space (all possible rules) for the best fit, while Inductive 
Bias (preferences for simpler rules) guides this search, preventing overfitting and enabling 
generalization to unseen data, making learning possible from limited examples.  
What is Concept Learning? 
 It's the process of inferring a general, often boolean-valued, function (the "concept") that 
describes a set of positive and negative training examples. 
 Example: Learning the concept of "play tennis" by seeing days where people play (positive 
examples) and days they don't (negative examples) to predict if someone will play on a new 
day. 
 It's framed as a search problem through a space of potential concept descriptions 
(hypotheses) to find the best one.  
How Hypothesis Space Influences Learning 
 Definition: The set of all possible hypotheses (rules or functions) that the learning algorithm 
can represent and consider. 
 Influence: The choice of hypothesis representation implicitly defines the hypothesis space. 
A smaller, more constrained space limits the complexity of what can be learned, while a 
large space offers more possibilities but makes finding the right concept harder.  
How Inductive Bias Influences Learning 
 Definition: The set of assumptions or preferences a learner uses to choose one hypothesis 
over others when multiple fit the training data. 
 Influence: 
o Necessary for Generalization: Without bias, a learner can't prefer a simple, general rule 
(like "all green apples are ripe") over complex, arbitrary rules that perfectly fit training data 
but fail on new data (overfitting). 
o Guides Search: It prunes the hypothesis space, guiding the search towards more plausible, 
simpler concepts (e.g., preferring linear models over highly complex polynomials), making 
learning efficient and effective. 
o Types: Can be explicit (e.g., preferring shorter logical expressions) or implicit (e.g., the 
inherent structure of a neural network).  
A-5. Explain the importance of data balancing techniques. Compare oversampling, 
undersampling, and synthetic balancing methods. 
Data balancing is like giving equal slices of cake to all classes so none gets grumpy. If one 
class is greedy, model will learn to always give it cake. Oversampling means copying the 
small class many times (clone the cupcakes) — easy but the cupcakes become many 
identical twins. Undersampling means eating some of the big class cupcakes so balance 
returns — but you lose tasty cake crumbs. Synthetic balancing (like SMOTE) is inventing 
new cupcakes by mixing recipes of two minority cupcakes, sometimes they taste strange. 
Balancing is important so the model does not learn to always vote for the loudest class at 
parties. Choose method depending on cupboard size and baking skill.
