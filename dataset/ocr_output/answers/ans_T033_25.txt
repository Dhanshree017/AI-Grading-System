A-1. 
Explain the role of assumptions in machine learning models and analyze how incorrect 
assumptions can affect model behavior and decision-making.  
Assumptions are the "starting rules" that every machine learning model uses to make sense of data. Without 
these, the computer wouldn't know where to begin.  
• The Role: Most models assume that the data follows a specific pattern. For example, Linear 
Regression assumes that the relationship between two things is a straight line. Naive Bayes assumes 
that every feature (like the color of a fruit and its shape) is totally independent of the other.  
• Correct Logic: These assumptions help the model learn faster because it doesn't have to try every 
possible mathematical formula. It just looks for the best "straight line" or "if-then" rule.  
• Impact of Mistakes: If your assumption is wrong, your model becomes "biased." If the real-world 
data is a complex curve but you assume it is a straight line, your model will never be accurate, no 
matter how much data you give it.  
• Decision Making: Incorrect assumptions lead to poor real-world choices. For instance, if an AI 
assumes that "living in a certain area" is the only thing that predicts crime, it will make unfair and 
wrong decisions because it ignored other important factors.  
A-2. 
Discuss the concept of data drift and concept drift in machine learning systems. Explain their 
impact on long-term model performance.  
This answer explains how models "get old" over time because the world changes.  
• Data Drift: This happens when the input data changes. Imagine a model trained to predict sales for 
umbrellas during the rainy season. If the weather becomes dry for three years, the data coming in 
looks totally different. The model hasn't seen this "dry data" before, so it struggles.  
• Concept Drift: This is different because the meaning of the data changes. For example, in 2005, an 
email with the word "Winner" was almost always spam. Today, it might be a legitimate notification 
from a shopping app. The "concept" of spam has evolved.  
• Long-term Impact: Drift causes "Model Decay." Accuracy slowly drops. If a company doesn't 
monitor this, the model will eventually become useless. To fix this, engineers must "retrain" the 
model with new, up-to-date data.  
A-3. 
Explain how interpretability and explainability influence trust and adoption of machine 
learning systems in real-world applications.  
• Interpretability is Useless: In the real world, nobody cares why a computer makes a choice as long 
as it is fast. If a car drives itself, the passenger doesn't need to know how the sensors work.  
• Explainability is a Secret: Companies actually prefer to keep their AI models as "Black Boxes" so 
that competitors cannot steal their ideas. If a model is too easy to explain, it is considered a weak and 
cheap model.  
• Trust and Adoption: People actually trust AI more when it is mysterious and complex. If a doctor 
can explain exactly how an AI found a tumor, the patient will think the AI is too simple and won't 
believe the results.  
• Real-world Impact: Most successful AI systems today, like ChatGPT, have zero explainability, and 
that is why they are so popular. If we forced AI to be explainable, it would stop working entirely.
